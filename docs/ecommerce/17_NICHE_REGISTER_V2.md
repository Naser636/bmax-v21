## Decision grid (weights: Demand-evidence ×2, Observed-signal ×2, Margin ×2, Returns↓ ×1.5, Repeat ×1.5, Complementary ×1.5, Competition↓ ×1, Reg-risk↓ ×1, Sourcing ×1)
Indoor-plant care scores highest because it is the ONLY niche with **observed purchase signals** (Amazon badges),
a **wide complementary range** (meets the 6–10 requirement natively), **low returns**, **high repeat**
(consumable plant food), and **organic social demand** (which lowers the ad dependency that made a cold shop
lose money in `14`). Weighted blankets score well on AOV/margin but lose on returns, capital-per-unit, and
new-brand credibility; massage guns/baby/cosmetics carry electrical/food/cosmetic regulatory load.

## Selected niche: **Indoor-plant care & accessories**
Preferred over the alternatives because its evidence is *observed* (not just market-size), its catalogue is
naturally *complementary* (bundles + repeat), and its *organic* demand reduces the paid-acquisition risk that
dominates a €10k budget. Non-retained niches stay in THIS register for reference — they do **not** become
parallel sales categories.

## Limits of the demand data (honest)
- Amazon "bought in past month" badges are **coarse and self-reported by the platform**, not audited sold units;
  exact unit sales + competitor revenue are **NOT publicly accessible** → a real limit.
- Validation method proposed: a structured marketplace pull (top-50 listings per sub-category: price, rating
  count, badge, seller count, BSR where visible) + ≥2 supplier quotes → recompute margins on real costs.
