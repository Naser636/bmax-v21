238. The Lightweight Brain Principle
ODG must remain:
SEMANTICALLY LARGE + OPERATIONALLY ADAPTIVE + PERMANENTLY SMALL.
The specification may be extremely detailed.
The capability universe may be extremely broad.
The Resolution Asset library may become very large.
The provider ecosystem may become very large.

But the permanent decision substrate remains the nine primitives.
The central rule is:
THE BRAIN SHOULD KNOW HOW TO COMPOSE CAPABILITIES, NOT CONTAIN EVERY CAPABILITY PERMANENTLY.
Therefore:
DO NOT build one permanent subsystem for every capability.
DO NOT create a permanent agent for every role.
DO NOT load every model.
DO NOT keep every provider active.
DO NOT keep every capability hot.
DO NOT keep every workflow resident.
DO NOT turn every successful experiment into permanent machinery.
Prefer:
INTENT → SEMANTIC COMPILATION → REQUIRED CAPABILITIES → ALLOCATION → TEMPORARY WORKGRAPH → EXECUTION → VERIFICATION → RETAIN WHAT IS VALUABLE.
This is how ODG can grow without becoming an usine à gaz.

240. Adaptive Governance Intensity
ODG uses an adaptive governance intensity model.
G0 --- ROUTINE / LOW RISK
Use when the action is:
reversible;
low value;
low blast radius;
within established policy;
within known capability;
well observed;
highly repeatable.
Behavior:
short planning;
lightweight verification;
automatic execution where authorized;
automatic recovery where proven;
no unnecessary human interruption.
Example:
formatting;
deterministic transformation;
known data normalization;
routine asset conversion;
repeated internal maintenance.
G1 --- STANDARD
Use for normal production or engineering work.
Behavior:
semantic mission compilation;
normal evidence;
standard verification;
bounded resource budget;
automatic retry/recovery where proven;
human escalation only on exception.
G2 --- ELEVATED
Use when:
uncertainty is meaningful;
cost is material;
external side effects exist;
quality is critical;
provider behavior is uncertain;
failure is expensive;
authority is more sensitive.
Behavior:
deeper verification;
stronger provenance;
champion/challenger where appropriate;
tighter budget;
explicit stop conditions;
human gate when required.
G3 --- CRITICAL
Use for:
irreversible actions;
major capital exposure;
legal/rights uncertainty;
security-critical actions;
constitutional changes;
material external authority;
high-blast-radius production changes.
Behavior:
explicit human authority where required;
independent verification where appropriate;
no silent fallback;
no autonomous promotion;
safe stop on unresolved critical uncertainty.
The system should default to the lowest governance intensity that safely satisfies the mission's actual risk.

241. Verification Must Also Be Adaptive
Verification is not binary.
ODG should not run the most expensive verification stack for every trivial action.
Canonical verification depth:
V0 --- SANITY
Basic schema/state/result consistency.
V1 --- STANDARD
Behavioral checks, expected output checks, provenance and normal regression.
V2 --- HIGH VALUE / HIGH UNCERTAINTY
Adversarial checks, deeper continuity, independent validation, stronger evidence.
V3 --- CRITICAL
Independent/external verification where required, explicit authority confirmation, full provenance, recovery proof and human approval where applicable.
The selection rule is:
VALUE + RISK + UNCERTAINTY + IRREVERSIBILITY + BLAST RADIUS → VERIFICATION DEPTH
A low-risk repetitive task should not be forced through a V3 process.
A high-risk task must not be allowed to use V0 merely to save time.
This keeps ODG both safe and usable.

defer one branch;

This creates a key rule:
DO NOT TURN UNCERTAINTY INTO DEADLOCK WHEN A SAFE PARTIAL RESOLUTION EXISTS.
