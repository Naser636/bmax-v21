## Concept 1 — "Quiet Car" (contained thriller / moral drama)
- **Hook:** On an overnight sleeper train, a grieving woman corners the man she's sure killed her brother in a
  hit-and-run — but the harder she pushes for a confession, the more the story bends, until the last stop
  reveals which of them has really been hunting the other.
- **Pitch:** A near-real-time two-hander across one night of track. Maren recognises Viktor from a years-old
  case file and begins a patient, deniable extraction of a confession before the terminus lets him vanish.
  Viktor doesn't deny, doesn't fear her — because the version of events she inherited from her family is a lie.
  The reversal is moral, not mechanical: she came to receive a confession and must instead give one.
- **Format/audience:** short film ~22 min; adult drama/thriller audiences; festival-friendly.
- **Self-assessment:** orig M-H · char H · drama H · dialogue H · visual/emotion M-H · ending H · feasibility H.
- **Risk:** the reversal must be *fair* (planted, not a cheat); confined setting demands dialogue that carries
  weight.

## Concept 2 — "The Understudy's Mother" (dance drama, memory)
- **Hook:** A former prima ballerina losing herself to early dementia is hired, half out of pity, to coach a
  faltering young dancer — until the "method" turns out to be the old woman re-living her own buried career,
  and a sabotage she has never confessed.
- **Pitch:** As the coach's memory frays, past and present blur; the young dancer realises she's being shaped
  into the rival the coach once destroyed. Stakes: an audition vs. a truth that could break them both.
- **Format/audience:** feature treatment ~95 min; arthouse/drama.
- **Self-assessment:** orig M · char H · drama M-H · dialogue M · visual/emotion H (dance/mirrors) · ending M-H ·
  feasibility M (choreography is hard to render in text; visual power lives off-page).
- **Risk:** the most cinematic element (dance) is the least expressible in a text dossier — weak fit for a
  *text* demonstration.

## Concept 3 — "The Cartographer of Debts" (magical-realist fable)
- **Hook:** In a town where every favour owed shows as a glowing thread on the skin, a young map-maker who can
  trade these debt-threads sets out to free her indentured father — and learns the town's whole prosperity
  rests on one unpayable debt owed to someone long dead, which calling in would collapse everything.
- **Pitch:** A moral fable about obligation and belonging, with a striking visual conceit (a society literally
  wired by debt) and a wrenching dilemma (free your father / spare the town).
- **Format/audience:** feature ~100 min; crossover literary/fantasy.
- **Self-assessment:** orig H · char M · drama M · dialogue M · visual/emotion H · ending M-H · feasibility M-L.
- **Risk:** world-building heavy; danger of concept outshining character; hardest to make *felt* rather than
  *explained* in a bounded dossier.

## Concept 4 — "Low Tide Radio" (coming-of-age road drama)
- **Hook:** Two teenage half-brothers who've never met — their late father kept two families — are made to
  drive his ashes across the country to a lighthouse he always talked about, guided by cassette tapes he left
  as directions, each one dismantling the father they thought they knew.
- **Pitch:** A dual coming-of-age and reluctant reconciliation; warmth, grief, and humour on the road; the
  father's voice on the tapes is the third character.
- **Format/audience:** feature ~100 min; broad arthouse/indie.
- **Self-assessment:** orig M · char H · drama M · dialogue H · visual/emotion M-H · ending M-H · feasibility M.
- **Risk:** road structure invites episodic sag/padding; the premise, while warm, is the most familiar.
