## LOGLINE
On an overnight sleeper train, a grieving archivist corners the stranger she's certain killed her brother in a
hit-and-run — but the closer she pushes for a confession, the more her story bends, until the last stop forces
her to face which of them has really been owed the truth all along.

## FORMAT JUSTIFICATION
**Short film, ~22 minutes, near-real-time, single location (one sleeper carriage + corridor).** The story is a
single engine — one night, two people, a terminus as a hard deadline — turning on one moral reversal. A feature
would have to graft on subplots that dilute that engine; the short's compression *is* the suspense. One location
and two principals also make it genuinely affordable to produce later (aligns with the honest economics in
`../07_MISSION_CINEMA_STUDY.md`). The runtime is set by the story (one night's ride), not by an arbitrary target.

## SYNOPSIS
MAREN VOSS, 34, boards the night train to the coastal terminus of Haldern. She is not travelling for a reason
she'll admit. In the near-empty sleeper she recognises a courteous older man, VIKTOR REINHOLT, 57, from a
photograph in a years-old case file: the hit-and-run that, she believes, killed her younger brother Tomas and
was never solved. She has one night, no proof, and a terminus that will let him disappear.

She works him patiently — a shared nightcap, a feigned coincidence, the archivist's trick of asking for a story
and listening for the seam. Viktor does not deny, does not fear her; he even helps, correcting a detail she has
wrong. That wrongness is the first crack. The car was not blue, he says. It was grey. He says it the way only
someone who was *there* can say a thing.

At the midpoint the hunt inverts. Viktor knows her name before she gives it; knows Tomas's name; knows the date
she's been circling. He is not a random driver she's cornered. He is the father of the girl her brother killed —
LENA REINHOLT, nineteen — the night Tomas, drunk, struck her on the coast road and fled, and the night the Voss
family's money and lawyers made it all go quiet. Tomas did not die at a stranger's hands. Two years later, hollowed
by what he'd done, he drove off that same road himself. Maren has spent years building a villain so she would not
have to build a verdict.

Viktor did not board to confess or to kill. He boarded because he had seen her letters to the cold-case unit, her
returns to the crash site — and he wanted, once, to watch her stop lying. At Haldern, in the grey before dawn, he
does not touch her. He sets a photograph of Lena on the table between them and steps off the train into the dark.
Maren is left alone in the emptying carriage, holding the face of the girl her family erased — and, for the first
time, she lets her brother be who he was.

## CHARACTER BIBLE
**MAREN VOSS — 34, archivist.** She spends her days ordering other people's records and has never been able to
open her own. *Want:* a confession that makes her brother a victim and her grief clean. *Need:* to stop
protecting a lie and grieve the real Tomas. *Contradiction:* the confession she chases will destroy the innocence
she's defending. *Arc:* prosecutor → penitent. Controlled, precise, dangerous in her patience; the cracks show in
what she won't say.

**VIKTOR REINHOLT — 57, retired marine engineer.** Courteous, exact, unbearably calm — a man who has rehearsed
this restraint for years. *Want (shown):* to reach Haldern. *Want (true):* to be *acknowledged* — to watch the
other family finally face it, and to set down a weight that has outlived his anger. *Contradiction:* years of
banked fury vs. an exhaustion that no longer wants revenge, only the truth spoken aloud. *Arc:* silent watcher →
the one who releases her, and himself. He keeps turning a small photograph in his pocket.

**THE CONDUCTOR — 60s.** Minor but load-bearing: the line's memory and the clock. He has seen Maren on this
train before, on this same pilgrimage she won't name. He announces the stops; he is the film's quiet witness.

## NARRATIVE STRUCTURE (three movements, no plot holes)
- **MOVEMENT I — Recognition (0:00–7:00).** Establish Maren's false calm and true purpose; the recognition;
  first contact; the deadline (Haldern, 05:40). Inciting beat: she decides to work him. Hook of doubt planted:
  her facts are all secondhand ("the file said," "they told me").
- **MOVEMENT II — The Crack (7:00–15:00).** The extraction. He doesn't behave like the guilty. Escalation to the
  midpoint reversal: "grey, not blue" → he knows her name → he is Lena's father. Stakes flip from *his* guilt to
  *hers*.
- **MOVEMENT III — The Reckoning (15:00–22:00).** The truth laid out: Tomas the driver, the cover-up, the
  suicide. Maren's collapse and defence. The denouement at Haldern: not revenge — a photograph, and release.
  Final image: Maren lets Tomas be who he was.

## SÉQUENCIER (scene-by-scene; dramatic purpose + visual intent)
1. **PLATFORM, NIGHT.** Maren boards without checking the departure board — she knows this train. Visual: she
   touches a folded newspaper clipping in her coat, doesn't read it. *Purpose:* she's done this before; dread
   under composure. (Setup 1, 6, 7.)
2. **SLEEPER CORRIDOR.** The Conductor clips her ticket: "Haldern again." She doesn't answer. *Purpose:* deadline
   + she's a repeat traveller she won't explain. (Payoff seed 6.)
3. **COMPARTMENT — RECOGNITION & FIRST CONTACT.** *[Fully dialogued below.]* She sees Viktor; matches his face to
   the file photo; engineers a conversation. *Purpose:* establish cat-and-mouse; plant that her facts are
   inherited; his odd calm; she deflects how Tomas actually died ("the coast road"). (Setup 1, 3, 4.)
4. **LOUNGE CAR.** A nightcap. She tests him with the "facts." He answers a half-beat too easily; he turns a worn
   photograph in his pocket, which she reads as a sentimental keepsake. *Purpose:* escalate; the audience starts
   to feel the wrongness; plant the photograph. (Setup 2, 3, 5.)
5. **CORRIDOR, LATER.** Beat of near-sympathy; she almost likes him; hates that she does. The Conductor passes:
   "Two hours to the coast." *Purpose:* humanise both; tighten clock.
6. **COMPARTMENT — "GREY" (MIDPOINT REVERSAL).** *[Fully dialogued below.]* She insists the car was blue; he
   corrects her — grey; then uses her name she never gave. The floor drops. *Purpose:* the hinge. (Payoff 2;
   turn.)
7. **LOUNGE CAR, EMPTIED.** He lays it out plainly: Lena; the coast road; the cover-up; the money. Maren
   prosecutes, denies, bargains. *Purpose:* exposition as confrontation, not narration; her defence is the drama.
   (Payoff 1, 4.)
8. **CORRIDOR / VESTIBULE.** She breaks — the suicide she's never said aloud. The train slows for Haldern.
   *Purpose:* lowest point; the truth she's hidden even from herself. (Payoff 4.)
9. **COMPARTMENT — HALDERN (REVELATION OBJECT).** *[Fully dialogued below — denouement.]* Not revenge. The
   photograph. He leaves. *Purpose:* earned moral reversal + release. (Payoff 5, 3.)
10. **CARRIAGE, DAWN.** Alone, Maren turns the clipping over at last and reads both names. First honest breath of
    grief. *Purpose:* resolution; the image the whole film was withholding. (Payoff 7.)

## DIALOGUED SCENES (pivotal — demonstrating dialogue, causality, reversal, denouement)

### SCENE 3 — INT. SLEEPER COMPARTMENT — NIGHT
*A narrow compartment. MAREN sits with a book she isn't reading. Across the aisle, VIKTOR does a crossword in
pen. She watches his face until she's sure. She closes her book.*

**MAREN:** You do those in pen.

**VIKTOR:** If I'm wrong, I've earned the mess.

**MAREN:** Most people want to be able to take it back.

**VIKTOR:** *(not looking up)* Most people haven't decided what they think.

*A silence. The track hums. She moves to the seat across from him — uninvited. He notices; allows it.*

**MAREN:** I can never sleep on these. My brother used to say the trick is to pick a point in the dark and let it
pull you. *(beat)* He took this line. Years ago.

**VIKTOR:** And you're retracing it.

**MAREN:** Something like that.

**VIKTOR:** *(finally looks at her)* People go to the coast to begin things or to end them. You don't look like
you're beginning.

*It lands harder than he could know. She holds her face still — the archivist's stillness.*

**MAREN:** You come up this way often?

**VIKTOR:** I used to. I'm getting reacquainted.

**MAREN:** With the line?

**VIKTOR:** *(a small, closed smile)* With the stops.

### SCENE 6 — INT. SLEEPER COMPARTMENT — DEEP NIGHT (MIDPOINT)
*They've been talking for an hour. She's circled close. She lays a photocopied page on the table — a page from
the case file — face down, like a card she hasn't played.*

**MAREN:** Eleven years ago. The coast road, outside Haldern. A car took a girl off the verge at night and kept
going. Blue car. They never found the driver.

**VIKTOR:** *(evenly)* Grey.

**MAREN:** ...What?

**VIKTOR:** The car was grey. Not blue. The paper got it wrong, and then everyone copied the paper.

*A pause. She has never once heard anyone contradict the file. Her certainty tilts.*

**MAREN:** The report says blue.

**VIKTOR:** The report was written by people who weren't standing on that road. I was.

**MAREN:** *(carefully)* You were a witness.

**VIKTOR:** I was the one who got there first. *(beat)* You can put the page away, Maren. I know what's on it.

*She goes very still. She never told him her name.*

**MAREN:** I didn't —

**VIKTOR:** Voss. Maren Voss. Your brother was Tomas. *(gently, which is worse than cruelty)* Sit down. You've
had this the wrong way round for eleven years, and you've come an awfully long way to keep it that way.

### SCENE 9 — INT. SLEEPER COMPARTMENT — PRE-DAWN (HALDERN — DENOUEMENT)
*The train slows. Lights of Haldern slide across their faces. Maren is wrecked and upright, the way archivists
are. Viktor takes a small, worn photograph from his pocket — the one he's been turning all night.*

**MAREN:** If you came to make me say it, I'll say it. My brother killed your daughter. My family paid so he
wouldn't answer for it. *(breath)* And when he couldn't live with it, he drove off that same road, and we called
it an accident so we wouldn't have to say the rest.

**VIKTOR:** I know. I've known for eleven years. *(beat)* I didn't get on this train to hear it. I got on because
I saw your letters to the cold-case unit. The ones you never sent to anyone who'd actually be hurt by them. I
wanted to see if you'd ever stop writing to strangers and look at it.

**MAREN:** *(barely)* What do you want?

**VIKTOR:** Nothing you can give me. *(he sets the photograph on the table between them — a laughing girl of
nineteen)* Her name was Lena. She did crosswords in pen. I'd like one person in your family to have seen her
face on purpose.

*The train stops. Doors hiss. He stands, straightens his coat with that terrible courtesy, and does not look
back.*

**VIKTOR:** Go home, Maren. Stop riding to the coast. There's nothing out there but where it happened.

*He steps down onto the platform and is gone into the grey. Maren does not follow. She looks at the photograph
for a long time.*

### SCENE 10 — INT. CARRIAGE — DAWN (FINAL IMAGE)
*The carriage empties. The Conductor passes, pauses — he's seen her ride this line a dozen times and never once
get off at Haldern.*

**CONDUCTOR:** End of the line, miss. *(gentler)* You getting off this time?

*Maren shakes her head — but differently than before. She unfolds the newspaper clipping she's carried all night.
For the first time we — and she — see it: a grainy photo of her brother, and beside it, in her own handwriting in
the margin, a name she has finally allowed onto the same page: "Lena Reinholt." She sets Lena's photo over the
clipping. She breathes out. The train sits still.*

**CONDUCTOR (O.S.):** *(as he moves on)* ...Right you are.

*FADE OUT.*

## ENDING — why it satisfies (not arbitrary)
Every setup is paid: the secondhand "facts" (S3) are wrong because they were sanitised (S6–7); "blue vs grey"
(S6) proves Viktor's firsthand presence; his calm (S3–4) is the calm of the wronged, not the guilty; the
unspoken "accident" (S1) resolves as the suicide (S8); the turned photograph (S9) becomes Lena's face; the
repeated pilgrimage (S2, S10) resolves when Maren finally *doesn't* need to ride anymore. The reversal is moral
rather than mechanical — the person demanding a confession is the one who must give one — and the denouement
withholds violence in favour of the harder thing: being made to see. It ends not on who gets punished but on who,
at last, tells the truth.
