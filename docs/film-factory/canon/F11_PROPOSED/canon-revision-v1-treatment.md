## 1. Approved direction (verbatim constraints)
- Protagonist = a **massive, muscular Viking** with an **enormous two-handed sword**.
- **Male characters carry the central dramatic, military, and action arcs.**
- **Exactly two** female characters remain, both **genuinely secondary**.
- **Comedy is mandatory**, emerging naturally from character + situation.
- Preserve cinematic quality, historical-fantasy mystery, escalating stakes, emotional consequence, feasibility.

## 2. The protagonist — BJÖRN STORMHAND (new lead)
- **Physique / signature weapon:** a giant of a man; two-handed greatsword **_Himinkljúfr_ ("Sky-Splitter")**.
- **Personality (beyond muscle):** deadpan, economical with words, a dry humour that lands hardest when the
  world is loudest; stubborn; fiercely loyal to crew; superstitious about the sea.
- **Motivation:** he crossed the storm-seam chasing the squall that drowned his younger brother; now trapped in
  an age of two suns, his goal narrows to one thing — get his people home and shut the door that is swallowing
  both worlds.
- **Real weaknesses (not cosmetic):**
  1. **Strength cannot solve the mystery.** A sword cuts men, not tides or time. His entire method fails against
     the seam — forcing him to *trust a reader* and to *learn to read*, which humbles the warrior. This is the arc.
  2. **Deep-water dread.** The brother he lost went under; Björn, a seafarer, is quietly terrified of drowning.
     The climax demands he face exactly that.
  3. **A slow berserker rage** he fears loosing, because last time it cost a life on his own side.
- **Combat signature (evolves — not just "big man hits"):** early, brute cleaving and using the greatsword as a
  terrain tool (planted as an anchor against a flood-surge; the flat sweeping a rank off shifting ground; the
  overhand "sky-splitter" that breaks a shield-wall). **Arc payoff:** he learns *restraint* — timing single,
  disciplined strikes to Astrid's tide-reads, fusing the male action arc with the reader's intel. His final,
  decisive act is **not** a swing.

## 3. The two secondary female characters (exactly two)
- **ASTRID** — the **tide-reader**, now a **genuinely secondary** mentor-of-knowledge and ally. She supplies the
  crucial intel and *teaches Björn to read*. She no longer carries the lead or the decisive climax. (Demoted from
  F10 protagonist — see role-reassignment audit; she must NOT be a relabeled lead.)
- **MERYT** — Egyptian river-guard captain, secondary ally and the **emotional origin** of the tear (her forbidden
  double dawn-rite, grieving a drowned brother, cracked the sky). Her guilt and partial redemption are a secondary
  emotional thread, not the spine.

All other female roles are removed or re-gendered to male (see §5): **IPY→IPU** (a boy with his late father's
water-jug) and **WIDOW→the WIDOWER** (an old man keeping a drowned neighbour's place). This is a canon-revision
transformation, logged — not a silent edit.

## 5. Mystery / time-travel premise — preserved, with ONE flagged incompatibility
Preserved recognizably: storm → the seam of gold light → an age of two suns → a debt that must be repaid or both
ages bleed together → the door closes only when **someone who can read it walks in and does not return**.

**Incompatibility (biggest):** F10's climax is **female-decisive** — Astrid reads the field and a reader closes
the door. The new direction requires the **protagonist (Björn)** to carry the decisive action. **Proposed
resolution (needs human sign-off — see incompatibility-register):**
- **Primary (B):** Björn's arc is *the warrior who learns to read*. By the convergence he has learned enough of
  Astrid's craft to become "someone who can read it," and **he** walks into the seam and does not return — the
  sacrifice is his, earned by his arc; the canon rule is preserved intact.
- **Alternative (A):** the rule is revised — the door needs a **life given AND a hand to hold it from within**;
  Björn plants _Himinkljúfr_ in the seam-root and holds it shut by main force while Astrid reads the timing; the
  seam takes him. (A rule change — flagged.)

## 6. Action redesign (varied, readable, consequential)
Keep the environmental-action signature (sea/seam/ground as the battlefield) but now **Björn is the physical
engine** and Astrid is the **intel**. Rules: no two set-pieces repeat (vary scale/purpose/Björn's control-level:
brute → tactical → disciplined → sacrificial); readable geography; **weapon continuity** (the greatsword is tracked
every scene — where it is, when it's planted, when it's lost/recovered); real physical consequences (fatigue,
wounds, drowning); tactical intelligence (Björn + Astrid as muscle+mind). Escalation ladder: SC01 storm → first
clashes → tide-aided skirmishes → the field at the seam-root → the sacrifice.

## 7. Comedy (mandatory, character-based)
- **Björn × Börk:** a warrior and a cook, two mountains of men, arguing war vs supper; Börk feeds everyone mid-crisis.
- **The greatsword as a running gag:** _Himinkljúfr_ won't fit in the longship, jams in doorways, Björn keeps
  clipping lamps/pots — physical comedy that also *characterises* a brute in a world too delicate for brute force.
- **Tove's gallows humour**; **Björn's deadpan** under impossible odds. Comedy sits **between** set-pieces as relief
  and never undercuts a death or the mystery's dread.
