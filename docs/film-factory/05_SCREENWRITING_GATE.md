**Rule (addendum §4):** do NOT accept self-evaluation by the same model as proof of quality. An **independent

## Independent critique (adversarial reader — DELEGATION, model-based, NOT human)
A separate agent instance (bounded provider delegation; declared + accounted; **not** ODG-native autonomy, and
**not** a human reader) was prompted as an adversarial development executive and given only the design file.
Result:

- **Score: 4/10. Verdict: NEEDS_WORK.**
- **Genuine strengths:** the water→sand hero image; objective-vs-objective antagonism (Nefer-Ka is *right*); the
  rule-bound, monotonic-desync invariant; a flaw that maps to the climax; a specific sound thesis.
- **Rejection-level weaknesses:** (1) ~50-min runtime is an unmarketable no-man's-land; (2) the sacrifice ending
  is pre-solved/telegraphed; (3) self-sacrifice-to-close is the genre's most exhausted ending; (4) "two worlds"
  stakes are faceless; (5) crew are furniture, Meryt is a function; (6) dialogue is on-the-nose/aphoristic.
- **Cliché risk (critical):** unacknowledged **Stargate** collision (ancient Egypt + sun/"Door" + temple
  aperture) — "weak differentiation, could be bounced on the logline." Also Mummy/Prince-of-Persia furniture.
- **On-the-nose lines flagged:** the opening V.O. proverb ("the sea… takes what it is owed") and the
  "Something else did" trailer-beat.

## ODG disposition (honest)
**The screenplay does NOT pass the professional gate** (4/10). The critique is accepted in full. Six targeted
structural revisions were applied at the design level (`02` §6 Revision 2): reframed as a **proof-of-concept
short**, **severed the Stargate iconography** (Door = an alignment phenomenon, not a temple), **contested
sacrifice** (Nefer-Ka can also seal it), **faced stakes** (named casualties Helgi + an Egyptian family),
**people not functions** (Meryt's private objective; named crew), and **cut the on-the-nose opening**.

## Acceptance criteria for passing the gate (set explicitly, before next draft)
1. A full **dialogued draft** of the (short) screenplay exists (not just design) — distinct from this design doc.
2. A **second independent critique reaches ≥ 7/10** AND confirms the Stargate collision is resolved.
3. Dialogue passes an on-the-nose check (no character states the theme as a proverb; subtext present).
4. Stakes include ≥1 **witnessed** death before the climax.
5. Ideally, a **human** reader confirms (model critique is a proxy, not a substitute).
