# La Porte du Soleil — Film Design & Pre-Production (text-tier feasibility proof)

**ODG_FILM_FACTORY_V1 · 2026-10-24 · ORIGINAL fantasy-adventure.** This is the producible proof: concept
critique + improved story design + production bible + a fully-dialogued opening sequence + its shot list.
Creative writing = bounded PROVIDER delegation (declared; NOT ODG autonomy); ODG verifies structure
deterministically (`03`). This is a **screenplay/pre-production artifact — NOT a film** (§10).

**Historical disclaimer:** this is original fantasy; every Norse and Egyptian detail is dramatized and is
distinct from historical fact (the ~2,000-year gap is the premise, via the time portal — not an error).

## 1. Critique of the base concept
Base: "A viking drakkar appears in Ancient Egypt; a captain, a young navigator, an Egyptian officer, and a
prince-guardian of an artifact face a time-portal threatening two worlds."
- **Too many co-leads** → no single objective/flaw/cost. Fix: ONE protagonist (the navigator), others orbit her.
- **Vague McGuffin + vague stakes** ("threatens two worlds"). Fix: make the Door's danger *visible and rule-bound*
  (a "time-tide" that desyncs both eras — sea floods desert, a second sun rises, moments echo).
- **Anachronism risk**: Vikings (~9th c. CE) vs pharaonic Egypt (~1200 BCE) are ~2,000 yrs apart — the **time
  portal is the premise that licenses this**, not an error; the film states openly it is fantasy, distinct from
  historical fact.
- **Antagonist**: avoid a cartoon villain. Make the prince-guardian *right* (his duty is to keep the Door sealed)
  so the conflict is objective-vs-objective, plus the impersonal Door as the real threat.

## 2. Improved story design
- **Logline:** When her storm-wrecked longship is thrown through a collapsing sun-portal into an impossible
  Egypt, a young viking navigator who never trusts her own instinct must choose between the only way home and
  the one sacrifice that can close the Door before it drowns two worlds in each other's time.
- **Narrative promise (first 30s):** a wave of seawater turns to **sand** mid-break and a **second sun** climbs
  the sky — the audience is told instantly this is a film about two times bleeding together.
- **Protagonist — ASTRID (19), navigator.** *Objective:* get the crew — and her dying father-captain — home.
  *Flaw:* she reads every star and map but defers her own instinct to the captain; she follows, never chooses.
  *Costly choice:* the Door can only be sealed from *inside* by a navigator who charts it — a one-way passage;
  closing it means never going home. *Arc:* follower of the map → the one who leaps.
- **Antagonist(s):** **NEFER-KA (30s), prince-guardian of the Door** — *right*: his sworn duty is to keep it
  sealed at any cost, which makes Astrid's crew the threat. And the **Door itself** — the impersonal "time-tide"
  with escalating, visible consequences.
- **Bridge — MERYT (30s), captain of the Egyptian river-guard** — pragmatic, forces the uneasy alliance;
  translator of worlds.
- **Emotional engine — OLAF, Astrid's father, the dying captain** — wants only to die at home; his wish vs the
  seal is the heart's dilemma.
- **Fantasy rules (coherent, visible):** (a) the Door opens only at solar alignment; (b) every crossing *borrows
  time* that must be repaid — the longer it stays open, the more both eras desync (twin suns, frozen waves, sand
  turning to sea, moments that echo); (c) it closes only when a living navigator charts it from within and seals
  it behind them — one way. Each rule pays off a set-up (see `03` setup→payoff).
- **Theme:** home vs duty; the map vs the leap.
- **Structure (~50 min, 3 acts):** ACT I (0–12') arrival + the Door + first desync; ACT II (12–36') uneasy
  alliance, rules learned, desync escalates, a reversal (Nefer-Ka sabotages the crossing to protect his world);
  ACT III (36–50') the sacrifice — Astrid charts the Door from inside, seals it, chooses *not* to go home; last
  image: the single sun, the sea back where it belongs, Olaf buried in Egyptian sand under a Norse cairn.
- **Visual identity (distinctive, non-imitative):** "Sun & Salt" — Norse cold steel-grey/sea-green vs Egyptian
  gold/ochre, meeting in a **bronze-green portal light**; signature effect = the **time-tide** (afterimages/
  echoes of people and waves). No servile imitation of any existing film.

## 3. Production bible (continuity rules)
- **Characters:** Astrid (braided ash-blond, star-chart tattoo on forearm — continuity anchor); Olaf (grey beard,
  iron arm-ring, failing); Nefer-Ka (shaved head, golden solar pectoral — the artifact is worn, not carried);
  Meryt (khopesh, bronze scale-corslet). **Faces/wardrobe locked via reference sheets** (to be generated in a
  tool with character-consistency, e.g. Midjourney V8.2 Edit — not available here).
- **Locations:** the storm-sea→desert threshold; the Sun-Temple of the Door (colonnade, solar disc aperture);
  the Nile-edge guard camp; the Door interior (a corridor of mirrored suns).
- **Props:** Astrid's sealing-chart; Olaf's arm-ring; the solar pectoral (the Door's key); the longship "Sōlfari".
- **Palette/light:** Act I cold→hot clash; Act II bronze-green creep as desync grows; Act III blinding gold then
  a calm single-sun dawn. **Continuity rule:** desync level must only ever increase until the seal (never flicker
  back) — a checkable invariant.
- **Sound:** Norse low drones + Egyptian double-reed (zurna-like) motifs that converge into one theme at the seal;
  the time-tide = a reversed-echo signature. Music/voice must be rights-cleared (ElevenLabs Music v2 / licensed).

## 4. OPENING SEQUENCE (spectacular) — fully dialogued
### SCENE 1 — EXT. NORTH SEA — STORM — NIGHT → IMPOSSIBLE DAY
*Black. A navigator's voice, close, steady over chaos.*
**ASTRID (V.O.):** My father says the sea has only one rule. It takes what it is owed.
*SMASH TO: the longship SŌLFARI climbing a mountain of black water. Rain like nails. OLAF lashed to the tiller,
grey and shaking. ASTRID clings to the mast, a star-chart in oilcloth pressed to her chest.*
**OLAF:** Astrid! The stars — give me the stars!
**ASTRID:** *(screaming over wind)* There are no stars! The sky's gone!
*She looks up — the storm-clouds are lit from beneath by a second, impossible glow. The great wave curls to
break over them —*
*— and at its peak the water turns to SAND. The wave collapses not into foam but into a roaring dune. The ship
slams down, timbers screaming, and grinds to a halt tilted in desert under a sky with TWO SUNS.*
*Silence, sudden and total. Sand hisses off the hull. The crew, a dozen battered Norse, stare.*
**OLAF:** *(barely)* ...Where have you brought us, girl?
**ASTRID:** I didn't. *(beat, looking at the twin suns)* Something else did.
### SCENE 2 — EXT. DESERT THRESHOLD — CONTINUOUS
*Hoofbeats. A patrol of the river-guard crests the dune — MERYT at the front, khopesh drawn, more bewildered
than hostile. Behind her, the silhouette of a temple with a hole of light where its sun-disc should be.*
**MERYT:** *(in Egyptian, subtitled)* Sea-ghosts. The Door has coughed up sea-ghosts.
**ASTRID:** *(raising empty hands, to her crew)* Spears down. Spears DOWN. Look at them — they're as afraid as we are.
*A low TONE rolls across the desert. Far off, a patch of sand shivers and — for one impossible second — becomes
WATER, reflecting the twin suns, then sand again. Meryt and Astrid both flinch toward it. Their eyes meet: the
first thing they share is fear of the same thing.*
**MERYT:** *(lowering the khopesh an inch)* You feel that too.
**ASTRID:** *(nodding slowly)* The sea's still owed.
*OFF Astrid's face — reading the temple's hole of light like a chart she almost understands — CUT TO TITLE:*
**LA PORTE DU SOLEIL.**

## 6. Revision 2 — design changes applied after INDEPENDENT critique (score 4/10 → NEEDS_WORK; see `05`)
An independent adversarial reader scored the above 4/10. The Rev-1 material above is PRESERVED; these are the
accepted structural pivots (the dialogue/full-draft work remains outstanding — the gate is NOT passed):
1. **Runtime reframed:** this is explicitly a **proof-of-concept SHORT (~8–12 min)**, not a 50-min film — 50 min
   was an unmarketable no-man's-land. Full-length is a *later* decision after the short validates.
2. **Sever the Stargate collision (critical):** the Door is **no longer a sun-temple aperture**. It is a
   *phenomenon at a solar alignment on open water/dune* — no built monument, no sun-disc hole — killing the
   iconographic rhyme with Stargate. Egyptian setting stays; the portal imagery becomes original (a seam of
   light where sea and sand interleave).
3. **Contested, not pre-ordained, sacrifice:** mid-Act-III reveal that **Nefer-Ka can also seal it** — Astrid's
   real choice becomes *let him die for her world* vs *take it herself*; the ending stops being arithmetic.
4. **Face the stakes:** a **named Egyptian family and a named Norse crewmate (HELGI)** become the first
   time-tide casualties by midpoint — one witnessed death, not "two worlds" in the abstract.
5. **People, not functions:** **Meryt gets a private, conflicting objective** (she is covering that her own
   guard's ritual *triggered* the alignment); ≥2 Norse crew (HELGI, TOVE) are named and want something.
6. **Cut on-the-nose theme:** the opening **V.O. proverb is removed**; open cold on the storm, let the
   water→sand image carry the premise; the "sea is owed" line survives **once**, late, from Olaf, when it costs
   him. "Something else did" trailer-beat cut.
These pivots are design-level; a full revised **dialogued draft** + a **second independent critique (must reach
≥7/10)** are required before the professional gate is passed (`05`).
