# LA PORTE DU SOLEIL — full-length screenplay (Draft F3)

**ODG_FILM_FACTORY_V1 · 2026-10-24 · target 60:00 · ORIGINAL fantasy-adventure.** F3 is the architectural rewrite
of F2 (`18`), addressing the independent feature critique (6/10): a **sustained antagonist** (Seth-Nakht planted
in Act I, escalating through a midpoint reversal into the climax), an **escalating — not repetitive — midsection**,
**Tove's arc paid off** (named: Halla), and a **de-aphorised** dialogue pass (aphorisms reserved for a few
load-bearing beats; no repeated arc-line). Story, arcs, and ending UNCHANGED. F1/F2 preserved as history. A
screenplay, **NOT a film**; needs a feature critique ≥7/10 (`19` stop-condition) + a human read before production.
Original fantasy; Norse/Egyptian detail dramatized, distinct from historical fact; no real persons.

**Principals:** ASTRID (19, navigator — follows, never chooses) · OLAF (60s, dying captain, her father) · HELGI
(20s, carries a wooden horse for his daughter Sǫlveig) · TOVE (20s, Astrid's closest friend; a woman named HALLA
waits for her on a grey beach) · MERYT (30s, river-guard captain; her forbidden dawn-rite tore the seam) ·
NEFER-KA (30s, reads & can seal the seam) · IPY (10, water-carrier, mother lost to the first tide) · SETH-NAKHT
(40s, garrison commander — sustained antagonist, fear-and-ambition driven) · the SEAM (impersonal antagonist).

# ACT I — ARRIVAL (0:00–15:00)

## SEQUENCE 1 — THE CROSSING (0:00–5:00)

### 1. EXT. NORTH SEA — STORM — NIGHT
*Black water the height of hills. The longship SŌLFARI claws up a wave, sail reefed to a rag. At the steering-oar:
ASTRID, 19, reading the sea the way other people read faces. Lashed to the mast behind her, OLAF, 60s, grey as
ash, too weak to stand but still her captain in every eye that darts to him for permission.*

**ASTRID:** She's pulling beam-on. I hold, she rolls. I fall off, I lose the next shoulder.

**OLAF:** *(thin)* And which does the sea want?

**ASTRID:** *(a flash of frustration)* I'm not asking what the sea wants. I'm asking you to tell me I'm right.

**OLAF:** *(not unkind, but done bending)* Ask the water, Astrid. I've answered you for the last time tonight.

*Stung, she holds the line she doubts. The ship rolls hard; sea comes over the rail. HELGI, 20s, ties off a
snapped sheet one-handed — a child's carved wooden horse wedged in his belt. TOVE, 20s, bails, swearing.*

**TOVE:** *(shouting, half a grin)* When we drown, I'm blaming the navigator's nerves, not the sea!

**HELGI:** *(to Astrid's back, not joking at all)* Then steer like he's already gone. You'll never move otherwise.

*ASTRID glances at OLAF — eyes shut, spending his last strength on breathing — and back at a wave now rising
beyond all the others, a black wall under the lightning.*

### 2. EXT. NORTH SEA — THE SEAM — CONTINUOUS
*The monster wave curls to break. At its crest, where there should be foam, the WATER UNRAVELS — a seam of hard
gold light splits it, and through the gap the sea is SAND, dune, a shore under TWO SUNS. For one impossible beat
Astrid sees both worlds stacked: storm above, desert below.*

**ASTRID:** *(under her breath, the sailor's prayer gone wrong)* ...I can't read that.

*The ship drops through. The storm cuts out mid-roar. The keel slams into SAND. Silence, total — rain still
dripping off the rigging, hissing on hot ground. Soaked men in a dry land under a doubled sun.*

**OLAF:** *(faint, eyes opening)* ...This isn't a shore I taught you.

**ASTRID:** No. *(staring at the twin suns)* This one I'll have to learn.

## SEQUENCE 2 — TWO SHORES, ONE COMMANDER (5:00–10:00)

### 3. EXT. IMPOSSIBLE SHORE — DAWN OF TWO SUNS — CONTINUOUS
*Behind the beached ship, a vertical SEAM of light hangs in the air, breathing, widening a finger each minute.
Hoofbeats. River-guard crest the dune: MERYT, 30s, khopesh drawn — and behind her, where no child should be, IPY,
10, a clay jug on her hip. ASTRID steps between her crew's blades and the patrol, hands open.*

**ASTRID:** We didn't cross to raid you. Your sea reached up and took us off ours.

*MERYT doesn't lower the blade — but her eyes keep going past Astrid to the seam, afraid of it, not of them.*

### 4. EXT. SHORE — SETH-NAKHT ARRIVES — CONTINUOUS
*A second, larger column — garrison soldiers, disciplined, and at their head SETH-NAKHT, 40s, commander, a man who
reads a crisis for what it can win him. He takes in the ship, the foreigners, the seam, and the fear on his own
captain's face, and he smiles the small smile of a man handed an opportunity.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(Egyptian, subtitled)* The sky tears, and the same morning strangers fall out of it. *(to his
soldiers, for them to hear)* The people will want to know which caused which. *(to Meryt)* Chain them. Gently, if
your conscience needs it. But chain them.

**MERYT:** Commander — they came *through* the tear. They did not make it.

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(mild, which is worse)* I did not ask what is true, Meryt. I asked what the town needs to see.
*(beat)* Bring them to the garrison. The sea-ghosts are mine now.

*ASTRID can't follow the words, but she reads the body language exactly — the commander has just made her crew his
property. She catches Meryt's eye; Meryt looks away, ashamed and outranked.*

### 5. EXT. SHORE — THE FIRST TIDE — CONTINUOUS
*A low TONE rolls across the dunes. Forty paces off, flat sand SHIVERS and becomes mirror-bright SEA for three
seconds, then collapses to dune. Soldiers flinch; IPY, fearless, drifts toward it.*

**MERYT:** IPY — back!

*ASTRID is already moving — she reads the water's run, cuts the angle, reaches the child as the false sea rises to
their knees, and hauls her clear onto dry dune. The sea drains. ASTRID stands soaked in a desert, shaking. SETH-
NAKHT watches — not grateful, calculating.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(quiet, to a lieutenant)* The ghost-girl knows where the water goes before it goes. *(beat)* A
useful animal. Keep it alive until it isn't.

## SEQUENCE 3 — THE GARRISON (10:00–15:00)

### 6. INT. GARRISON — HOLDING YARD — DAY
*The Norse held under guard in a walled yard. OLAF laid in shade, failing. NEFER-KA, 30s, shaved head, a plain
solar pectoral, is brought in — not a prisoner, but not free either; Seth-Nakht keeps the kingdom's seam-guardian
close. He studies the distant seam with a stillness that uncannily mirrors Astrid's way of reading water.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(to Astrid, nodding at the seam, ignoring the guards)* You read water. I have spent my life reading
*that.*

**ASTRID:** Then tell me how to make it give my father's ship back.

**NEFER-KA:** It is an alignment — the two suns draw close, the wall between their ages thins, things fall through.
The debt must be repaid or both ages bleed together until neither keeps its own rules. *(beat)* It closes only
when someone who can read it walks into it and shuts it from within. It does not return that someone.

*ASTRID files "from within" too fast. OLAF, nearby, hears it, and his eyes find his daughter with fear.*

### 7. INT. GARRISON — SETH-NAKHT'S CHAMBER — DUSK
*SETH-NAKHT, NEFER-KA, MERYT. The commander turns a captured Norse brooch in his fingers like a man pricing it.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** You have guarded that tear your whole life, Nefer-Ka, and it has never once torn like this. Why now?

**NEFER-KA:** The alignment makes a seam possible. But this one opened wide, and early, before the suns had met.
That is not the alignment. That is a hand.

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(looking straight at Meryt)* A hand. *(lets it hang)* Whose?

**MERYT:** *(holding, barely)* The priests would know better than a river-captain, Commander.

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(smiling)* Mm. *(to Nefer-Ka)* Find me the hand. A guilty hand is a gift — the people forgive a
ruler anything if he gives them someone to blame. Foreign ghosts will do until you find better.

*He has, without knowing it, named Meryt's crime as the thing that will save his standing. NEFER-KA sees her flinch.
He says nothing — yet.*

### 8. EXT. GARRISON WALL — NORSE, NIGHT
*The crew against a wall under guard. TOVE whets a blade; HELGI turns the wooden horse.*

**TOVE:** She'll get us out. She reads her way out of everything.

**HELGI:** She reads. She doesn't *choose* — not without the old man's eyes open behind hers. And they're closing.

**TOVE:** *(quiet, a crack in the bravado)* There's a woman on a grey beach north of Hǫrn who thinks I drowned a
year ago. Her name's the one I say to the sea when it gets bad. *(beat)* I'd like to not have drowned, Helgi.

**HELGI:** *(showing the horse)* Sǫlveig. Six winters. I've not been home since the night she was born. *(closes
his hand)* So we get the navigator choosing. Fast.

### 9. INT. GARRISON — OLAF'S CORNER — NIGHT
*ASTRID with OLAF, a torch guttering. The seam faintly visible through a high window.*

**OLAF:** I heard the guardian. "From within." I saw your face find a route and hide it.

**ASTRID:** I haven't hidden anything.

**OLAF:** You've hidden things from me your whole life to spare me, and I let you, to spare you. *(a breath that
costs him)* Don't read that seam for my sake, Astrid. Not to carry me home to cold ground. If you ever walk into
a thing you can't walk out of, let it be your reason. Not mine.

*ASTRID doesn't answer — which is its own answer. Through the window, the seam widens. A soldier's shadow falls
across them: Seth-Nakht's watch, always there now. END ACT I.*

# ACT II — THE BLEED (15:00–42:00)

## SEQUENCE 4 — THE COERCED ALLIANCE (15:00–20:00)

### 10. INT. GARRISON — SETH-NAKHT'S TERMS — MORNING
*SETH-NAKHT has ASTRID brought before him, MERYT translating, NEFER-KA present. He does not offer freedom. He
offers a leash.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(via Meryt)* My guardian says the tear widens and will drown my towns. You see where water will
go. *(beat)* So you will walk the shore beside him and keep my people dry, and in return I will not feed your crew
to the thing to calm a mob. That is the whole of the kindness I have.

**ASTRID:** And my father? My ship?

**SETH-NAKHT:** Stay here. Under my roof. *(a thin smile)* Call them my guests. A man keeps his guests close when
he wants a borrowed animal to come home at night.

*ASTRID understands every inch of it without the words. She looks at OLAF, held; at her crew, held; and nods once,
hating it. NEFER-KA watches her swallow the leash and says, low, only to her:*

**NEFER-KA:** He keeps what you love so your gift stays his. Learn the seam faster than he thinks you can. It is
the only key he hasn't counted.

### 11. EXT. DESERT — SEAM-READING, UNDER GUARD — DAY
*NEFER-KA teaches ASTRID the seam's grain. A soldier shadows them, reporting back. She learns fast — reads a tear
before it opens, saves a shepherd. But every time she succeeds, the soldier rides to tell Seth-Nakht, and her
value — and her cage — grows.*

**ASTRID:** *(reading, eyes shut)* It leans east... then it jumps — there.
*A dune ripples to sea where she pointed. NEFER-KA, quietly amazed, then grim:*

**NEFER-KA:** Every time you prove how well you read, you make yourself too valuable to ever let go. *(beat)* I
have guarded this seam thirty years and I have never been free of it either. He only made your chain visible.

## SEQUENCE 5 — THE MARKET, AND THE CREDIT (20:00–26:00)

### 12. EXT. RIVER-TOWN MARKET — DAY
*IPY shows ASTRID her world — proud, alive. HELGI and TOVE trail under guard, foreign and wary. IPY points to a
flat paving stone.*

**IPY:** *(Egyptian, subtitled)* My mother sold sweet water there. The first morning the sea walked, it walked
here. It took the corner. And her. *(sets her jug straight on the stone)* I keep her place.

*ASTRID catches "first morning" — this has happened before — and clocks Meryt's flinch across the square.*

### 13. EXT. MARKET — THE SECOND TIDE, AND SETH-NAKHT'S THEATRE — CONTINUOUS
*The TONE — deeper. ASTRID reads it in a heartbeat.*

**ASTRID:** *(roaring, pointing)* The east row — get them OFF it, NOW!

*Hesitation — then MERYT bellows it in Egyptian and the crowd scatters as the east row BECOMES SEA, stalls
spinning under. Where Astrid read clear, people live. The water drains. But SETH-NAKHT is already there with
heralds, stepping into the saved square as if he conjured the rescue —*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(to the crowd, arms wide)* Your commander's foreign hounds smell the flood before it comes! Fear
nothing while I hold their leash!

*The crowd cheers HIM. ASTRID, soaked and shaking, watches a man take the thing she did and wear it. This is not a
repeat of the first tide — it is the moment she learns the cage has a public lock, too.*

**ASTRID:** *(to Meryt, low, hard)* He didn't save them. I did. You did.

**MERYT:** *(quietly)* Here, the one who is seen to save them owns them. Remember that when it matters.

## SEQUENCE 6 — MIDPOINT: THE LEVERAGE (26:00–31:00)

### 14. INT. GARRISON — THE SEIZURE — NIGHT
*Word has reached SETH-NAKHT from Nefer-Ka: the seam opened early and wide — a guilty hand. He acts, and the floor
drops out of the film. Soldiers drag the SŌLFARI's gear into the yard; OLAF's litter is moved under heavier guard.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(to Astrid, via Meryt, pleasant)* My guardian tells me the tear was forced open by a hand, and
that it closes only when a life is given to it. *(beat)* I have decided the life will be a *guilty* one — the hand
that opened it. The people will love me for the justice of it. *(leans in)* You will take me and my guardian to
the tear's root at the convergence. You will read us there safely. And if your reading falters, or your crew grows
brave, your father does not see another of these ugly double dawns.

*ASTRID's face. The leash just became a noose around OLAF. She looks at MERYT — who now knows Seth-Nakht means to
throw the "guilty hand" into the seam, and who knows whose hand it is.*

### 15. INT. GARRISON — ASTRID & MERYT — LATER
*The two women alone, the stakes total.*

**MERYT:** He means to find the guilty hand and feed it to the seam at the convergence. He thinks a guilty life
closes it cleaner.

**ASTRID:** Does it? A guilty life?

**MERYT:** *(a beat too long)* It closes when someone who can *read* it shuts it from within. Guilt is Seth-
Nakht's idea, not the seam's. *(looks away)* But if he needs a guilty hand to throw, I could spare a great many
people by being the hand he throws.

**ASTRID:** *(sharp)* That's not a plan. That's your grief looking for a door.

*MERYT doesn't deny it. The seed of her possible sacrifice — and of the real one — is planted.*

## SEQUENCE 7 — THE CANYON (31:00–36:00)

### 16. EXT. DESERT CANYON — DUSK
*The march to the seam-root, under guard, through a canyon where the bleed is worst: waves of seawater hang FROZEN
mid-break, bronze-green, over dry rock. Norse, guard, Seth-Nakht's soldiers, OLAF's litter, IPY on TOVE's
shoulders. Beauty and dread.*

**NEFER-KA:** Walk only where I walk. Here the world has forgotten which age it stands in.

**TOVE:** *(craning up at a frozen wave)* If I die in this place, I die owing a woman in Hǫrn an explanation.
*(to Astrid)* You're going to get us to her. Say it.

**ASTRID:** *(can't lie to Tove)* I'm going to try to get *you* to her.

*TOVE catches the pronoun. She doesn't push — but she heard it.*

### 17. EXT. CANYON — THE ECHO — CONTINUOUS
*A time-echo rolls through. For seconds each person walks beside a translucent DOUBLE a few heartbeats behind.
ASTRID's double, behind her, mouths OLAF's words from scene 9: "...let it be your reason. Not mine." She falters;
NEFER-KA steadies her. Meanwhile SETH-NAKHT's own echo shows HIM, older, alone on a throne of sand — and he
flinches from it, the first human thing we've seen him do.*

**NEFER-KA:** The echoes show what you are closest to losing. Don't let them choose your moment for you.

## SEQUENCE 8 — THE COST (36:00–39:00)

### 18. EXT. SEAM-ROOT APPROACH — THE THIRD TIDE — DAY
*The root rears ahead: a vertical wound of light. HELGI hauls an anchor line to steady the crossing. The TONE —
the biggest surge. The false sea rises between HELGI and dry ground, chest-high in a breath.*

**ASTRID:** HELGI — drop it, take my hand, REACH —

*A stride too far, and he knows it. He looks — not at her hand — at the wooden horse in his belt. He tears it free
and THROWS it to ASTRID.*

**HELGI:** Get her home — Sǫlv—

*The sea takes him. Gone. Dry dune. The horse in Astrid's fist. TOVE screams into a silence that doesn't answer.
SETH-NAKHT, unmoved, to a lieutenant: "The tear is hungry. Good. It will take the guilty one all the easier."*

### 19. EXT. SEAM-ROOT APPROACH — AFTER — CONTINUOUS
*ASTRID doesn't collapse. She pockets the horse. Her face sets into OLAF's tiller-focus. TOVE, wrecked, grabs her.*

**TOVE:** He threw it to you. Not his own hand. Why—

**ASTRID:** Because he'd already chosen who the horse was for. *(beat, barely holding)* He stopped trying to save
himself and saved the thing that mattered. *(looks at the seam)* I didn't understand that this morning. I do now.

## SEQUENCE 9 — THE TURN (39:00–42:00)

### 20. EXT. SEAM-ROOT CAMP — NIGHT — THE CONFESSION
*The suns almost one. SETH-NAKHT assembles everyone: he will name the guilty hand and hold it for the convergence.
He points at the Norse — the easy scapegoats. MERYT steps forward and takes it out of his mouth.*

**MERYT:** *(ringing)* The hand was mine, Commander. My brother drowned; I lit the dawn-rite a second time, for
him, forbidden, to beg the river back. The suns touched twice. I tore the sky. Not these foreigners — me.

*A stunned beat. ASTRID expects the confession to free her crew. Instead SETH-NAKHT SMILES — he has been handed
exactly what he wanted.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(delighted, cold)* My own captain. Even better — the people will see I spare no one, not even my
own. *(to soldiers)* Hold her for the tear. At the convergence, the guilty hand goes in. *(to Astrid)* And you
will read her safely to the heart so my justice does not miss.

### 20A. EXT. SEAM-ROOT CAMP — IPY & MERYT — NIGHT
*MERYT bound, waiting for the convergence and the death Seth-Nakht has staged for her. IPY slips past the guards
and crouches in front of her — the child whose mother drowned in the first tide Meryt's rite called down. A
reckoning, not a comfort.*

**IPY:** *(Egyptian, subtitled, flat and level — the straight look Olaf praised)* The first morning the sea
walked. That was you. Your second flame. *(beat)* My mother.

**MERYT:** *(she does not look away; she has stopped looking away)* ...Yes. Your mother. And every other corner
it took. Me.

**IPY:** *(a long child's silence, then)* Seth-Nakht wants to throw you in for it. *(beat)* I don't want you
thrown in. I want you to stand on the shore where she stood, every day, and not look away from it. That's longer
than drowning.

*MERYT takes it like a blow she's owed. IPY sets her mother's jug down beside the bound woman — not forgiveness,
but a sentence — and goes. (F3.1 — the one reckoning scene; critic's highest-impact note.)*

### 21. EXT. SEAM-ROOT CAMP — THE TRAP CLOSES — CONTINUOUS
*MERYT seized. OLAF held. The crew held. ASTRID is to be forced to walk the one person who confessed into the
seam, for a commander's theatre — believing a guilty sacrifice is needed, which Astrid now knows is a lie.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(low, to Astrid, urgent)* He has it wrong. The seam does not want guilt. It wants a reader, and it
keeps them. If Meryt walks in, she dies for his story and the seam still needs a reader after. *(beat)* There are
only two people here who can read it to the heart. Her hand is not one of them.

**ASTRID:** *(the whole trap clear now)* Then he's about to spend the wrong life. And keep spending them until he
spends the right one.

*The two suns slide close enough to blur toward a single coin of light. Soldiers everywhere. END ACT II.*

# ACT III — THE SEAL (42:00–60:00)

## SEQUENCE 10 — CONVERGENCE UNDER SIEGE (42:00–48:00)

### 22. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — THE SUNS CONVERGING — DAY
*The two suns are nearly one searing coin. The seam is a vertical wound taller than the mast, roaring softly. The
desert flickers constantly between sand and sea. SETH-NAKHT's soldiers ring the root; MERYT is bound; OLAF's
litter and the crew are held as leverage. SETH-NAKHT, drunk on the theatre to come, gives the order.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(via a herald, to the gathered guard)* At the joining of the suns, the guilty hand goes into the
tear, and your commander closes the wound that his own captain opened! *(to Astrid)* Read her path to the heart.
Now.

**ASTRID:** *(not moving)* If I walk her in, she dies and the seam stays open — because she can't read it shut.
You'll have spent a life for a story and still be standing here when the next dawn doubles.

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(a flicker of doubt, buried)* Then I will spend another. And another. I have a garrison of hands.

**NEFER-KA:** *(stepping out)* You have two readers, Commander. Her, and me. Spend us and you have none — and no
one to shut it the day it opens over your own house.

### 23. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — THE STANDOFF — CONTINUOUS
*A knife-edge. ASTRID reads the grain going still — the convergence window is opening. She makes her move not with
a blade but with the thing only she can do: she calls the next three tides, out loud, to the inch, and they come
exactly as she says — sea erupting and draining around the soldiers, who break ranks in terror. In the chaos TOVE
cuts MERYT free; the crew take OLAF's litter back.*

**ASTRID:** *(to Seth-Nakht, over the roar)* You never held the leash. The water did. And it only ever answered me.

### 24. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — SETH-NAKHT'S CHOICE — CONTINUOUS
*SETH-NAKHT, his soldiers scattering, his theatre collapsing, draws his blade on the nearest hostage — OLAF — to
force Astrid back under his heel. And then he sees it: across the flickering ground, his own time-echo from the
canyon — the old man alone on a throne of sand. For one beat the commander looks at who he becomes. He lowers the
blade a fraction — then, as a surge rises toward the crew, he does the first ungoverned thing in the film: he
shoves OLAF's litter clear of the water himself, and turns on his own panicking soldiers.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(hoarse, to his men, no theatre left)* Hold the line for the foreigners. HOLD IT. *(quiet, to
himself)* ...Not a throne of sand. Not me.

*He is not redeemed — he spent lives to get here — but he chooses, visibly, with his hands, to stop being the man
on the throne. He throws his soldiers into holding back the tides so the readers can work.*

## SEQUENCE 11 — THE CONTESTED SEAL (48:00–54:00)

### 25. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — TWO READERS — CONTINUOUS
*The window is open. NEFER-KA ties back his robe and steps toward the wound of light — his duty, his age, his
death. ASTRID steps into his path. She doesn't argue. She kneels, sets HELGI's wooden horse upright in the sand,
and lays two fingers to the stilled seam, then points to a dim fold in the glare.*

**ASTRID:** The heart's there. Not where it's brightest — where it's quiet.

*The seam pulses once, confirming her to the inch — the dim fold NEFER-KA has guarded thirty years and never once
heard another name.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(undone)* ...Thirty years. You found it on your first convergence.

**ASTRID:** I never once trusted the water under my own feet. I'm done not trusting it. *(a look at him, level)*
Your world needs a reader *standing on this shore* for the next tear. Not lost in the light. That's you. It was
always going to have to be you who stays — so it can't be you who goes.

*Her hand rests on the wooden horse. She doesn't say the rest. NEFER-KA looks from her hand to the heart she alone
found, and steps back — the hardest obedience of his life.*

**MERYT:** *(reaching her, raw)* It should be me. I opened it. Let me be the one who —

**ASTRID:** Your grief opened it. Your life won't close it — you can't read it, and I won't spend you on Seth-
Nakht's idea of justice. *(gentle, final)* Live. Guard it with him. That's the harder sentence, and it's yours.

## SEQUENCE 12 — WHAT THE SEA IS OWED / SINGLE SUN (54:00–60:00)

### 26. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — OLAF — CONTINUOUS
*ASTRID crosses to OLAF, nearly gone, eyes opening clear one last time.*

**ASTRID:** I can send you all home through the seam before I shut it — the ship, Tove, the grey sea. Or I can
close it. Not both. The light keeps the reader who shuts it.

**OLAF:** *(the faintest smile)* The sea takes what it's owed, girl. Owe it me — not you. *(his hand finds hers)*
I spent your whole life being the eyes behind yours. Close it. Live on the far side. And say it out loud, once, so
I hear you choose before I go.

**ASTRID:** *(steady — no tremor, the first fully unforced choice of her life)* I'm closing the seam. I'm staying.
You go home without me. I chose it. No one told me to.

*OLAF's hand tightens — pride, complete — and stills. She presses her forehead to his. She does not weep yet. She
rises, pressing Helgi's horse into TOVE's hands.*

**ASTRID:** Take him to the grey sea, Tove. Take the crew. Take this to Sǫlveig — your hands, not mine. *(beat)*
And the woman on the beach in Hǫrn — Halla — tell her you didn't drown. Tell her to her face.

**TOVE:** *(breaking, fierce)* I'll tell her you're the reason I got to. *(grips her)* Read your way to something
good over here, Astrid. Promise me a thing for once in your life.

**ASTRID:** *(the ghost of Olaf's smile, hers alone)* I promise I'll choose it.

### 27. EXT. SEAM / SHORE — THE CLOSE — CONTINUOUS
*TOVE and MERYT drive the SŌLFARI at the seam. INTERCUT: the ship with the crew and OLAF's body aboard strikes
the wound of light and is PULLED through — released backward into grey storm, cold spray, home; TOVE at the oar
with the wooden horse, screaming Astrid's name once into the crossing. ASTRID steps into the wound. INTERIOR OF
THE SEAM: a corridor of stilled suns, every surface a mirror of a moment she's leaving — Olaf young at a tiller,
Helgi tossing Sǫlveig skyward, a grey beach. Each one whispers: turn, run, go home. She reads through them to the
quiet heart and closes her hand, sealing the seam shut behind the vanished ship.*

### 28. EXT. SHORE — THE SUNS PART — DAWN
*The single coin splits. One sun sinks for good. The false seas drain and do not return. An ordinary dawn — one
sun — on an ordinary shore. The seam is gone. ASTRID stands alone, alive, exiled from her own age forever. One
choice, and it was the whole of her.*

### 29. EXT. THE CAIRN — SINGLE-SUN MORNING
*A grey-stone cairn on gold sand — built by Egyptian hands for a Norse captain whose body went home without it.
MERYT sets the last stone. IPY lays a desert flower. SETH-NAKHT stands apart, helmet in hand — a commander who
came to feed strangers to the sea and ended holding the line for them; not forgiven, but no longer the man on the
throne of sand. NEFER-KA watches the horizon he still guards.*

**MERYT:** *(beside Astrid)* What do your people call a navigator with no sea left to cross?

**ASTRID:** *(a long beat; the first true calm of her life, looking at the thread of the great river)* We don't
have a word for it. *(beat)* But that river runs further than anyone here has followed it. Someone should learn to
read it.

**MERYT:** Further than anyone's dared.

**ASTRID:** *(turning inland, not looking back for anyone's nod)* Then it had better be me.

*IPY falls in beside her, uninvited, the way the brave do. HOLD: the two of them small against the long bright
river, one honest sun above. CUT TO BLACK.*
