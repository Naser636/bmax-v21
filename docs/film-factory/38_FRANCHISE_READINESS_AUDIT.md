## 1. The current ending — does it stand alone? **YES, completely.**

**[FACT] What the ending actually is** (F10 sc33–38A, lines 792–896, read in full, not presumed):
- **Main conflict resolved, permanently.** Astrid closes the seam (sc35–37); "The single coin splits. One sun
  sinks for good. The false seas drain and do not return… The seam is gone." The world-collision that drove the
  film is **over and cannot cheaply recur** — the door is shut by design.
- **Protagonist arc complete.** Astrid ("follows, never chooses") makes "the first fully unforced choice of her
  life," spoken aloud at Olaf's request: *"I'm closing the seam. I'm staying… I chose it. No one told me to."*
  (sc33). The 38A button closes it: she teaches Ipy to read, *"Don't look at me after to see if you're right.
  Just be right."* — the arc theme paid off in action.
- **Mentor resolved.** Olaf dies by his own framing ("The sea takes what it's owed… Owe it me — not you"), proud,
  on screen (sc33).
- **Antagonist resolved.** Seth-Nakht: "not forgiven, but no longer the man on the throne of sand" — he holds the
  line for the foreigners (sc30/38). A complete turn, not a loose thread.
- **Supporting arcs closed.** Meryt's guilt (she lit the second dawn-rite) is faced and she lives, wounded and
  bonded (sc26/30/38); the crew is sent home through the seam before it shuts (sc34–35); Börk's death gives the
  climax a real cost (sc30A).
- **Final emotion earned.** Astrid is "alive, exiled from her own age forever," lets herself weep once, then walks
  into "a whole living world she chose." CUT TO BLACK on the title.

**Closure-rule verdict:** The film is **comprehensible, emotionally complete, and satisfying with no sequel ever
produced.** Nothing essential is deferred. The "beginning" it lands on (the river, Ipy) is a *tone*, not an
unanswered question — the audience leaves whole. This is the governing constraint for everything below:
**no sequel may retroactively require the audience to have "waited."**

## 3. What must stay SECRET / un-foreclosed for the sequels

These are the load-bearing ambiguities. Film 1 must **not** explain or close them (and today's audit confirms it
does not):
1. **Astrid's mother** — who she was, why Astrid can't remember her waking. [FACT: sc36 shows her; explains
   nothing.] Reserve as a possible origin thread.
2. **Whether other seams/tears exist elsewhere** — keep it a single whisper ("the tear the day after"; Nefer-Ka's
   horizon). Do NOT confirm in film 1; confirming it would retroactively imply Astrid's seam wasn't the end.
3. **The home-world's fate** (Tove, Sǫlveig, Halla, the crew on the grey-sea side) — left offscreen on purpose.
   Reserve as a possible parallel-story thread. **Hard constraint:** the seam is *closed*, so any return to that
   world must be a *parallel* story on the other side, never a reunion-through-the-seam (that would betray the
   sacrifice).
4. **Where the great river leads** — the explicit forward-question Astrid chooses. The one seed meant to be felt;
   still, film 1 answers nothing about what she finds.

**Rule for all sequels:** respect the closed door. The seam does not reopen. Astrid's exile is permanent and
earned. Sequels extend *forward* (the river, the next generation, new boundaries) — never *undo*.

## 4. Plausible map of films 2–5 — **[HYPO] hypotheses, no production commitment**

Each volet: distinct premise · character evolution · new stake · organic link to film 1 · dramatic reason to
exist. None is commissioned; each must independently pass the same closure rule (stand alone) before it could ever
be greenlit.

- **Film 2 — "The River That Runs Further" [HYPO].** *Premise:* Astrid and Ipy follow the great river inland into
  a culture/polity none of them know. *Evolution:* Astrid, who finally learned to *choose*, must now learn to
  *lead and teach* — she becomes the Olaf. *New stake:* a boundary that is political/cultural, or a different
  natural anomaly — **not** a time-seam (that phenomenon is spent). *Organic link:* sc38/38A river quest + Ipy as
  apprentice. *Dramatic reason:* tests whether a navigator who "follows, never chooses" can bear the opposite
  burden — being the one others follow. **Strongest organic link.**
- **Film 3 — "Read Me the Next Bend" [HYPO].** *Premise:* years on; Ipy is now the navigator, Astrid aging.
  *Evolution:* the handoff of sc38A made literal — the apprentice must act without "looking back for the nod."
  *New stake:* Ipy's first solo catastrophe. *Link:* 38A's lesson, inverted. *Reason:* generational theme; proves
  the gift outlives the giver. Risk: needs Astrid present enough to matter yet absent enough to force Ipy — a
  casting/aging constraint to flag early.
- **Film 4 — "The Grey-Sea Saga" [HYPO] (weakest link — flagged honest).** *Premise:* parallel story on the
  home-world side — Tove's return, Sǫlveig, Halla, the legend of the captain who stayed. *Evolution:* Astrid seen
  only as myth/absence. *New stake:* a home-world conflict where "Astrid's choice" is a story people live by.
  *Link:* sc34's send-home. *Reason:* the mirror — what the sacrifice *cost* the ones saved. **Constraint:** must
  never reopen the seam; Astrid cannot appear in person. Because the link is thematic rather than plot-causal,
  this is the least organic of the four and should be treated as optional / anthology-style.
- **Film 5 — "The Door the Day After" [HYPO] (capstone).** *Premise:* the "tear the day after" was real; seams
  are a recurring cosmic fact; an older Astrid (or Ipy) must decide whether closing is always right. *Evolution:*
  the theme turns on itself — is *reading* and *closing* a form of control she once fled? *Link:* sc28 whisper +
  Nefer-Ka's horizon + the mother thread (possible reveal). *Reason:* thematic resolution of the whole saga.
  **Constraint:** only works if films 2–4 kept the "other seams" question a whisper.

**Saga shape [HYPO]:** 1 = the choice; 2 = the leadership; 3 = the inheritance; 4 = the cost (optional mirror);
5 = the meaning. A 2-film arc (1→2) is the smallest viable extension; 3–5 are escalating options, not promises.
