# ACT I — ARRIVAL (0:00–15:00)

## SEQUENCE 1 — THE CROSSING (0:00–5:00)

### 1. EXT. NORTH SEA — STORM — NIGHT
*Black water the height of hills. Rain like nails. The longship SŌLFARI claws up a wave, sail reefed to a rag. At
the steering-oar: ASTRID, 19, soaked to the bone, reading the sea the way other people read faces. Lashed upright
to the mast behind her, OLAF, 60s, grey as ash, too weak to stand but still her captain in every eye that darts
to him.*

**ASTRID:** She's pulling beam-on, Father. I hold this line, she rolls. I fall off, I lose the next wave's shoulder.

**OLAF:** *(barely over the wind)* Then you already know more than I do. Choose.

**ASTRID:** I'm not asking you to choose. I'm asking you to tell me I'm right.

**OLAF:** *(a sad, worn smile)* That's the same thing, girl. It's always been the same thing with you.

*She grips the oar, jaw set — and holds the line she doubts. The ship rolls hard; the sea comes over the rail in a
green wall. Crew scatter for handholds. HELGI, 20s, ties off a snapped sheet one-handed, a child's carved wooden
horse wedged safe in his belt. TOVE, 20s, bails with a split bucket, swearing like a sailor twice her age.*

**TOVE:** *(to Helgi, shouting)* If we die out here I'm telling the gods it was the navigator's nerves, not the sea!

**HELGI:** *(grim humour)* Tell them what you like. *(beat, to Astrid's back)* Astrid! Whatever you mean to do —
do it like he's already gone. That's the only way you'll ever do a thing in your life.

*ASTRID hears it. It lands like a slap. She looks at OLAF — eyes closed now, conserving the last of himself — and
back at the wave rising ahead, bigger than any before it, a wall against the lightning.*

### 2. EXT. NORTH SEA — THE SEAM — CONTINUOUS
*The monster wave curls to break over the ship. At its crest — where there should be foam — the WATER UNRAVELS. A
seam of hard gold light splits the wave down its face, and through the seam the sea is not sea: it is SAND, dune,
a shore, under a sky holding TWO SUNS. For one impossible instant Astrid sees both worlds stacked — storm above,
desert below.*

**ASTRID:** *(a whisper the storm eats)* ...That's not possible to read.

*The ship drops through the seam. The storm cuts out mid-roar. The keel slams into SAND, timbers shrieking, and
the SŌLFARI grinds to a tilted halt on a desert shore. Dead silence, sudden and total. Rain still drips from the
rigging onto hot sand, hissing. The crew lift their heads, soaked men in a dry land under a doubled sun.*

**OLAF:** *(eyes opening, faint)* ...Where have you steered us, Astrid?

**ASTRID:** I didn't steer this. *(staring at the twin suns)* Nothing I know how to read did this.

## SEQUENCE 2 — TWO SHORES MEET (5:00–10:00)

### 3. EXT. IMPOSSIBLE SHORE — DAWN OF TWO SUNS — CONTINUOUS
*Behind the beached ship, the seam still hangs in the air — a vertical scar of light the height of two men,
breathing, a finger's width wider each minute. The crew climb down, weapons half-drawn, disbelieving. Hoofbeats.
A patrol of the river-guard crests the dune: MERYT, 30s, bronze scale-corslet, khopesh drawn — and too close
behind her, where no child should be on a patrol, IPY, 10, a clay water-jug on her hip.*

**MERYT:** *(Egyptian, subtitled)* Ipy. Get behind me and stay there.

**IPY:** *(Egyptian)* They came out of the air, Meryt. Like the morning the sea walked.

*MERYT shoots her a look — too much said. ASTRID steps forward, open hands, putting her body between her crew's
blades and the patrol.*

**ASTRID:** *(slow, clear, knowing they won't understand the words but will read the hands)* Spears down. All of
you — down. *(to Meryt)* We didn't cross to your shore to raid it. Your sea reached up and took us off ours.

*MERYT doesn't lower the khopesh, but she doesn't raise it either. Her eyes keep going past Astrid — to the seam.*

### 4. EXT. SHORE — THE FIRST SIGN — CONTINUOUS
*A low TONE rolls across the dunes, felt in the teeth more than heard. Forty paces off, a stretch of flat sand
SHIVERS and — for three full seconds — becomes seawater, mirror-bright, reflecting the two suns, then collapses
back to dry dune. Every Norse hand tightens. MERYT and ASTRID both take an involuntary step toward it, the same
step, before catching themselves.*

**MERYT:** *(low, almost to herself)* ...Wider than yesterday.

**ASTRID:** You keep saying that. 'Yesterday.' 'The morning the sea walked.' *(beat)* This has been happening.

**MERYT:** *(guarded)* The desert has always had its moods.

**ASTRID:** The desert doesn't turn to ocean in front of a child and call it a mood. *(watching her)* You're not
afraid of us. You're afraid of that. So am I. That's the only honest thing between us right now — let's not waste it.

*A long beat. MERYT, grudging, lowers the khopesh a hand's width — a truce measured in inches.*

### 5. EXT. SHORE — OLAF CARRIED DOWN — CONTINUOUS
*TOVE and HELGI carry OLAF on a makeshift litter down from the tilted ship. He is failing fast. IPY, fearless,
drifts close to look at the grey old foreigner as if he were weather.*

**IPY:** *(Egyptian, to Olaf, matter-of-fact)* You're dying.

**OLAF:** *(who doesn't need the words translated; he smiles)* ...Aye. You're the first one brave enough to say it
to my face in a month. *(to Astrid, nodding at the child)* Keep that one close. She looks at things straight.

## SEQUENCE 3 — THE RULES AND THE LIE (10:00–15:00)

### 6. EXT. GUARD CAMP, NILE-EDGE — DAY
*The ship's survivors, uneasy guests, in the shade of the guard's awnings. NEFER-KA, 30s, shaved head, a plain
solar pectoral worn close to the skin, stands apart studying the distant seam with a stillness that uncannily
mirrors Astrid's own way of reading water. He does not look at Astrid when he speaks; he looks at the seam.*

**NEFER-KA:** You read water. *(beat)* I have spent my life reading that.

**ASTRID:** Then teach me to read it. Because it just threw my father's ship across the world and I would like to
throw it back.

**NEFER-KA:** *(the ghost of respect)* It is an alignment. Once in a long age the two suns draw close enough to
touch, and the wall between their times goes thin as a held breath. Things fall through. *(turns to her at last)*
The sea you fell through is a debt. It must be repaid. And the longer the wall stays thin, the more each age
bleeds into the other — until neither remembers which rules it is supposed to keep.

**ASTRID:** How is the debt repaid? How does it close?

**NEFER-KA:** Someone who can read the seam walks into it and closes it from within. *(flat, no drama)* The seam
keeps that someone. It does not give them back.

*ASTRID absorbs 'from within' — too fast, filing it like a route. OLAF, on his litter nearby, hears it too, and
his eyes find his daughter with something between fear and recognition.*

### 7. INT. GUARD TENT — DUSK — SAME DAY
*NEFER-KA and MERYT alone. He pours water; her hand, taking the cup, is not steady, and he notices everything.*

**NEFER-KA:** The seam opened at the suns' touch. That much is the alignment, and no one's fault. *(beat)* But it
opened *wide*, and it opened *early*, before the suns had fully met. That part is not the alignment, Meryt. That
part is a hand.

**MERYT:** *(too quickly)* Omens are the priests' concern. The river is mine.

**NEFER-KA:** I didn't say omen. I said hand. *(lets the silence work)* When I was a boy they taught me that the
old dawn-rite — light offered to the suns at the turning — is lit once, and once only, and never for the dead. To
light it a second time, for grief, to call back someone the river took... *(he watches her flinch)* ...that is
the kind of hand that tears a thin wall wide.

**MERYT:** *(whisper, not a confession yet, but the edge of one)* ...You should tend your seam, guardian, and
leave my grief to me.

**NEFER-KA:** Your grief flooded a market this week. It is not only yours anymore.

### 8. EXT. SHORE — NORSE FIRE — NIGHT
*The crew around a driftwood fire that burns a wrong, greenish colour. TOVE whets a blade; HELGI turns the wooden
horse over and over in his fingers.*

**TOVE:** You think she gets us home?

**HELGI:** She can read anything that moves on water. *(beat)* She just won't believe her own eyes unless her
father's are open behind them. And his are closing.

**TOVE:** *(quieter)* I've got a reason to get back, Helgi. I don't say it out loud because saying it out loud out
here feels like tempting the sea. But I've got one.

**HELGI:** *(showing her the horse)* Her name's Sǫlveig. Six winters old. I carved this the night she was born and
I've not been home since. *(closes his hand on it)* So have I.

### 9. EXT. SHORE — OLAF'S LITTER — NIGHT
*ASTRID sits with OLAF, the seam a faint gold wound on the dark horizon behind her.*

**OLAF:** I heard the guardian. 'From within.' *(beat)* I saw your face do the thing it does when you've found a
route and haven't told anyone yet.

**ASTRID:** I haven't found anything.

**OLAF:** You're the worst liar I ever raised. *(a breath that costs him)* Don't you dare read that seam to spare
me a grave in gold sand. I've had my sea. *(beat)* Promise me you won't choose it for my sake. Only ever for your own.

**ASTRID:** *(not promising, which is its own answer)* Sleep, Father. We'll talk routes in the morning.

*OLAF studies her a long moment — he knows her — then lets his eyes close. OFF Astrid, the seam widening behind her
in the dark — END ACT I.*

# ACT II — THE BLEED (15:00–42:00)

## SEQUENCE 4 — UNEASY ALLIANCE (15:00–20:00)

### 10. EXT. GUARD CAMP — MORNING OF TWO SUNS
*A tense parley in the hard double light. NORSE on one side, GUARD on the other, NEFER-KA and ASTRID in the gap,
MERYT holding the middle like a woman standing on a cracking floor.*

**NEFER-KA:** I can read the seam. I cannot stand at every place it tears at once. *(to Astrid)* You read where
water will go before it goes. I saw you take that step yesterday — the same step I took. Between us, we keep it
from drowning a town before I can close it.

**ASTRID:** And the price? You said it keeps whoever closes it. When that hour comes, who walks in?

**NEFER-KA:** I do. It is my seam, my age, my debt. That is settled. *(beat)* Teach your crew the tides. I will
teach you the grain of the light. Nothing more is owed between us than that.

**TOVE:** *(low, to Astrid)* We help him shut the one door that leads home.

**ASTRID:** We help him keep that child off the bottom of a sea that shouldn't exist. Home we fight for after.
*(beat)* Or do you want to explain to the gods that we let a town drown to save ourselves a walk?

*TOVE has no answer. The truce holds — barely.*

### 11. EXT. DESERT — SEAM-READING LESSON — DAY
*NEFER-KA traces the hanging light with two fingers; ASTRID mirrors him, then overtakes him — reading ahead, not
waiting to be shown. For the first time in the film she is not glancing over her shoulder for approval.*

**NEFER-KA:** It has a grain, like wind on water, like weather with a temper. You feel where it leans?

**ASTRID:** *(eyes half-closed)* It leans east... holds... and it'll jump — *there.*
*A far dune ripples to sea precisely where she pointed, then drains. NEFER-KA stares, unsettled by how fast she's
taken it.*

**NEFER-KA:** Who taught you to read like that?

**ASTRID:** No one. *(beat)* That was always the problem. I could read anything. I just never trusted it without
my father's nod. *(looks at the seam)* He's running out of nods.

**NEFER-KA:** *(quietly)* Then the seam may be the first thing you ever read for yourself. Mind it doesn't also
be the last.

## SEQUENCE 5 — THE MARKET (20:00–26:00)

### 12. EXT. RIVER-TOWN MARKET — DAY
*A crowded market at the Nile's edge — colour, noise, ordinary life. IPY, proud, tugs ASTRID between the stalls,
showing off her world. HELGI and TOVE trail, foreign and wary, half-charmed despite themselves.*

**IPY:** *(Egyptian, subtitled)* My mother sold water from this corner. The sweet water, from the deep well.

**ASTRID:** Sold? Where is she now?

**IPY:** *(simply, without self-pity)* The morning the sea first walked, it walked here. It took the corner. And
her. *(points at a flat paving stone)* I keep her jug. Meryt says I shouldn't stand on the low ground, but this
is where she was, so.

*ASTRID looks at the child, then across the market to MERYT, who has heard, and who cannot meet her eye. The
weight of 'the morning the sea first walked' settles on Astrid — and on us.*

### 13. EXT. RIVER-TOWN MARKET — THE SECOND TIDE — CONTINUOUS
*The TONE — deeper than before, wrong in the bones. ASTRID's head snaps up; she reads the grain of it in half a
second.*

**ASTRID:** *(roaring, pointing)* OFF THE LOW GROUND! It comes through the east row — MOVE THEM, NOW!

*A beat of fatal hesitation — a foreign woman screaming in a tongue they don't know. Then MERYT throws her
authority behind it, bellowing the order in Egyptian, hauling stallholders bodily uphill. The market square
BECOMES SEA — stalls lift and spin, a cart goes under — exactly along the east row Astrid named. Where she read
it clear, people stand dry and shaking on high ground. The water drains. No one dead. Barely.*

**MERYT:** *(beside her, chest heaving, awed and ashamed at once)* A whole quarter lived because a sea-ghost
shouted a warning in a language none of them speak.

**ASTRID:** They lived because you made them listen. *(turns on her, gentle but relentless)* Ipy said the sea
*first* walked and took her mother. 'First.' You keep flinching at the word 'twice.' I've stopped believing the
desert has moods, Meryt. Tell me what you did. Not for me. For the next market.

*MERYT opens her mouth — and can't, yet. She turns away. But it's cracking.*

### 14. EXT. MARKET AFTERMATH — IPY — CONTINUOUS
*In the wreckage, IPY calmly rights her mother's jug on the dry paving stone. ASTRID crouches to her.*

**IPY:** You knew where it would go. Like you'd already seen it.

**ASTRID:** I read it. The way my father taught me to read the sea at home. *(beat)* You read things straight too.
Your mother's in how you do that.

**IPY:** *(considering this, then, the highest compliment she has)* ...You'd have liked her corner. Before.

## SEQUENCE 6 — THE CANYON OF STILL WAVES (26:00–32:00)

### 15. EXT. DESERT CANYON — DUSK
*To reach the seam's root they must cross a canyon where the bleed is worst: great waves of seawater hang FROZEN
mid-break, glassy and bronze-green, suspended over dry rock. Beautiful. Deeply wrong. The band threads between
them — Norse and guard, IPY riding on TOVE's shoulders now, the two crews knitting into one.*

**NEFER-KA:** Walk only where I walk. Here the world has half-forgotten which age it stands in. A wrong step and
it may decide you belong to the sea.

**HELGI:** *(craning up at a frozen wave the size of a hall)* My gran swore the drowned see the sea stand still
forever. *(to Tove, wry)* Reckon we're the drowned, standing here looking at it?

**TOVE:** *(unsettled)* Don't. Out here a joke like that might come true.

### 16. EXT. CANYON — THE ECHO — CONTINUOUS
*A time-echo rolls through the canyon like a slow wave. For several seconds, each person walks beside a
translucent DOUBLE of themselves, a few heartbeats behind — their own near-future or near-past. ASTRID's double,
walking behind her, mouths words in OLAF's voice: '...only ever for your own.' She stops dead. NEFER-KA grips her
arm and pulls her forward.*

**NEFER-KA:** Don't watch the echoes. They show you what you're closest to losing — never what simply is. The
seam uses them to make you flinch at the wrong moment.

**ASTRID:** *(shaken)* Mine spoke in his voice.

**NEFER-KA:** Then yours is honest, at least. Most people's lie.

### 17. EXT. CANYON FLOOR — NIGHT — THE CONFESSION
*Camp among the still waves. ASTRID and MERYT keep a watch together, apart from the others. The frozen sea glows
faintly above them.*

**ASTRID:** 'Twice.' A child told me the suns touched twice the morning this began. An alignment is once. You
light a dawn-rite, don't you. For the guard. At the turning.

**MERYT:** *(a long silence; then the dam breaks — quiet, not dramatic, which is worse)* My brother went out on
the river in flood and it kept him. No body. No cairn. Nothing to grieve but water. *(beat)* The morning of the
turning I lit the rite as my duty asks. And then — forbidden, every priest would say forbidden — I lit it a
second time. For him. To ask the river, just once, to give something back. *(looks at her hands as if they belong
to a stranger)* The suns touched twice. The sky tore wide and early. Every ghost that has walked out of that sea
walked out of *my* grief, Astrid. Ipy's mother drowned in the flood I called down trying to un-drown my brother.

**ASTRID:** *(no cruelty, only weight)* And you let a stranger be the brave one. You let me shout the warnings so
you wouldn't have to say why I knew to.

**MERYT:** I let a stranger be braver than I was. *(meets her eyes)* I'm trying to stop. Starting now, with you,
in a canyon full of standing water, which is the only confessional I deserve.

**ASTRID:** *(after a beat)* Then stop by helping me close it. That's the only apology water accepts.

## SEQUENCE 7 — THE COST (32:00–37:00)

### 18. EXT. SEAM-ROOT APPROACH — THE THIRD TIDE — DAY
*The seam's root rears ahead: a towering vertical wound of light. To anchor the crossing, HELGI hauls a heavy line
against the wind. The TONE comes — the biggest surge yet. The false sea rises between HELGI and dry ground,
chest-high in a heartbeat, roaring.*

**ASTRID:** HELGI — drop the line! Take my hand — REACH!

*He is a stride too far, and he knows it in his face. He looks — not at her hand — at the wooden horse in his
belt. The one thing that has to get home to a six-winter girl named Sǫlveig. He wrenches it free and THROWS it,
not toward safety for himself, but to ASTRID.*

**HELGI:** *(as the sea takes his footing)* Like he's already gone — ! Get her home, Astr—

*The sea-that-was-sand pulls him under and is simply gone. Dry dune. Flat. Helgi not there. The wooden horse lands
in ASTRID's hand. TOVE screams his name into a silence that doesn't answer. IPY hides her face in Meryt's side.*

### 19. EXT. SEAM-ROOT APPROACH — AFTER — CONTINUOUS
*ASTRID does not collapse. She looks at the little horse in her fist. Something in her face hardens into the exact
focus her father had at the tiller. NEFER-KA watches the change with grief and recognition.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(gently)* That is the price the seam takes for every hour it stays open. Now you have held it in
your hand.

**ASTRID:** *(flat, quiet, final)* Then it doesn't get to take another one. *(pockets the horse)* Show me the root.

**TOVE:** *(hollow, to Astrid's back)* He threw it to you. Not his hand. The horse. Why would he —

**ASTRID:** *(not turning, barely holding)* Because he already knew what I'm only just learning. *(beat)* Some
things you choose for, even when choosing costs you the reaching.

## SEQUENCE 8 — THE TURN (37:00–42:00)

### 20. EXT. GUARD GARRISON — NIGHT — SETH-NAKHT
*Fear has spread through the river-towns: the ghosts came, the seas came, the two are one in the people's mouths.
SETH-NAKHT, 40s, a high officer above Meryt, arrives with armed soldiers — a man ruled by the need to be seen to
act.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(Egyptian, subtitled)* The omens began when the northern ghosts fell out of the sky. The people
want them given back to the sea that spat them up. Put them in irons. Feed the tear its foreigners and it will close.

**NEFER-KA:** That is not how it closes. You will drown a town to look decisive.

**SETH-NAKHT:** I will let the town sleep one night without screaming. That is a kind of ruling you have never had
to do, guardian.

### 21. EXT. GARRISON YARD — THE BREAK — CONTINUOUS
*Soldiers move to seize the Norse. TOVE's hand goes to her blade — it will be a slaughter. MERYT steps into the
open ground between the spears and the foreigners, and chooses, out loud, the thing she has hidden since Act I.*

**MERYT:** *(ringing, to Seth-Nakht and every soldier)* The omens did not begin with them. They began with ME. My
rite. My grief. My forbidden second flame at the turning. *(beat)* If this tear is owed a guilty hand, mine is the
only one in this yard that qualifies. Take me, if you must take someone. But these foreigners are the single
reason your market still has a quarter and your river still has an Ipy. Chain the brave ones and you will have
only me and the sea left when the suns meet.

*Stunned silence. SETH-NAKHT, braced to punish strangers, is not braced for a confession from his own captain.
The soldiers look to him; he has no script for this.*

### 22. EXT. DESERT — ESCAPE UNDER CONVERGING SUNS — NIGHT
*In the hesitation, ASTRID feels the grain of an oncoming tide. She doesn't fight the soldiers — she uses the
seam. She leads both crews, IPY, and a freed MERYT across a strip of ground she has read will flood in ninety
seconds, timing it so the false sea rises behind them, a wall of impossible water cutting the soldiers off without
a single blow struck.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(running at her shoulder)* You turned the thing that is killing us into a gate.

*ASTRID doesn't answer. She just reads the next pulse and adjusts their line without breaking stride — Helgi's
horse at her belt, her father's focus in her eyes. The change is in her hands now, not her mouth. (Her arc's
verbal articulation is held for the climax, scene 27/30 — F2.1 fix, feature critique §3.)*

*They vanish into the dunes toward the seam-root as, behind them, the two suns slide close enough to blur into one
blinding coin of light. END ACT II.*

# ACT III — THE SEAL (42:00–60:00)

## SEQUENCE 9 — CONVERGENCE (42:00–47:00)

### 23. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — THE SUNS CONVERGING — DAY
*The two suns are a single searing coin now. The seam is a vertical wound of light taller than the ship's mast,
roaring softly. The desert around it flickers constantly between sand and sea, unable to decide. The band
arrives, spent. OLAF's litter is set down in the lee of a dune.*

**NEFER-KA:** When the suns become fully one, the seam opens to its widest — and that is also its weakest. That
is the only moment it can be shut. *(beat)* And the moment is short. Shorter than it takes a tired man to sleep
and wake.

**ASTRID:** How do I know the instant?

**NEFER-KA:** You will feel the grain go still. For one breath the light stops lying. You read it then, or not
at all.

### 24. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — PREPARATIONS — CONTINUOUS
*Controlled chaos. MERYT and TOVE ready the SŌLFARI — dragged this far on rollers — bow aimed at the seam, to
send the crew home through it before the close. SETH-NAKHT arrives at the dune's crest with his soldiers — and,
after a long beat weighing the fear he rode in on against what he has seen, he swings down and sets his own
shoulder to the ship's stern, barking at his men to haul, not to seize. (F2.1 fix, feature critique §4 — his
change shown, not narrated.) IPY helps, small and fierce. NEFER-KA, apart, quietly ties back
his robe, the way a man does when he has decided to walk into something and not walk out.*

**MERYT:** *(to Astrid, low, watching Nefer-Ka)* He's said goodbye to no one. That's how you know he means to be
the one who walks in.

**ASTRID:** He's been ready to die for this since before my ship fell out of his sky.

**MERYT:** Ready isn't the same as owed. *(beat)* His people need a guardian who's still breathing when the suns
part. A dead guardian is just another ghost for the next grief to misuse.

### 25. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — TOVE'S GOODBYE — CONTINUOUS
*TOVE pulls ASTRID aside, Helgi's wooden horse in her own hand now.*

**TOVE:** The reason I wouldn't say out loud. It's a who. She's waiting on a grey beach for a ship that's a year
late. *(presses the horse into Astrid's hand)* If you put us through that seam — you give Helgi's Sǫlveig her
horse yourself, when you follow. You are following, Astrid. Tell me you're following.

**ASTRID:** *(can't lie to her, won't)* Take the horse to Sǫlveig, Tove. You, not me. *(beat)* Read that however
your heart needs to for the next five minutes. Then raise the sail.

## SEQUENCE 10 — THE CONTESTED SEAL (47:00–52:00)

### 26. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — THE SUNS BECOME ONE — DAY
*Blinding white. The grain of the seam goes glass-still — NEFER-KA feels it and steps toward the wound of light.
ASTRID steps into his path. She does not argue. She kneels, sets HELGI's wooden horse upright in the sand between
them, and lays two fingers to the air, reading the stilled seam. Then she points, flat, certain.*

**ASTRID:** There. The seam's heart is there — not where the light is brightest. Where it's quietest.

*NEFER-KA looks where she points — the dim fold in the light he has guarded his whole life and never once heard
anyone else locate. The seam PULSES once, confirming her to the inch.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(quietly, undone)* ...You read its heart. On your first convergence. I needed thirty years.

**ASTRID:** I never once trusted the water under my own feet. *(beat)* I'm done not trusting it. *(a look at him,
level)* Your world needs its guardian standing on this shore when the suns part. Not lost in that light where
nobody can read the next tear you'd have caught.

*Her hand rests on the wooden horse. She does not say the rest — that this is the first thing in her life she has
chosen with no one's nod to lean on. NEFER-KA looks from her hand, to the dim heart of the seam she alone found,
and — the hardest obedience of his life — steps back.*

**NEFER-KA:** Then read it all the way to the heart. Don't flinch at the middle. The middle is where it shows you
everyone you're leaving, to make your hand shake. Let it show you. Keep reading.

## SEQUENCE 11 — WHAT THE SEA IS OWED (52:00–56:00)

### 27. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — OLAF'S LITTER — CONTINUOUS
*ASTRID crosses to OLAF. He is nearly gone, but his eyes open — clear, for the last time.*

**ASTRID:** I can send you home. The ship, Tove, the crew, through the seam before I shut it. The grey sea, a
grave in cold ground, everything you asked. *(breath)* But I can't take you home *and* close the seam. The light
keeps whoever reads it to the heart. I can do one. I can't do both.

**OLAF:** *(the faintest smile)* The sea takes what it's owed, girl. *(beat)* Owe it me. Not you. *(his hand finds
hers)* I spent nineteen years teaching you to read, and the whole time I was the thing you read instead of the
water. Close the seam. Live on the far side of it. And say it out loud, once, so I hear you choose before I go.

**ASTRID:** *(steady — no tremor, the first fully unforced choice of her life)* I'm closing the seam. I'm staying
in this world. You're all going home without me. *(beat)* I chose it. Me. No one told me to.

*OLAF's hand tightens — pride, pure and complete — and then stills. She presses her forehead to his for a moment.
She does not weep. Not yet. She rises.*

**ASTRID:** *(to Tove and Meryt, raw but clear)* Put him through the seam with the ship — the grey sea should have
him, not gold sand. Build him a cairn at home. *(to Tove)* Helgi's horse goes to Sǫlveig from your hand. Go. Now.
The heart won't hold still long.

## SEQUENCE 12 — SINGLE SUN (56:00–60:00)

### 28. EXT. SEAM / SHORE — THE CLOSE — CONTINUOUS
*TOVE and MERYT drive the SŌLFARI at the seam. INTERCUT: the ship with the crew and OLAF's body aboard strikes
the wound of light and is PULLED through — released backward into grey storm, cold spray, home. From the shore,
IPY waves at a ship vanishing into another world.*

*ASTRID steps into the wound of light. INTERIOR OF THE SEAM: a corridor of stilled suns, every surface a mirror
of a moment. As she reads toward the dim heart, the seam shows her its lies — OLAF at the tiller, whole and young;
HELGI tossing Sǫlveig in the air; a grey beach, a life she could still turn back for. Each one says: flinch, turn,
run home. She does not flinch. She reads through them to the quiet heart — and closes her hand, sealing the seam
shut behind the vanished ship.*

### 29. EXT. SHORE — THE SUNS PART — DAWN
*The single coin of light splits. One sun sinks below the dunes for good. The false seas drain from the desert a
final time and do not return. An ordinary dawn — one sun — rises on an ordinary shore. The seam is gone. ASTRID
stands alone on the sand: this side, alive, exiled from her own age forever. One choice. It was the whole of her.*

### 30. EXT. THE CAIRN — SINGLE-SUN MORNING
*A small grey-stone cairn on gold sand — the wrong stones in the right shape — built by Egyptian hands for a Norse
captain whose body went home without it. MERYT sets the last stone. IPY lays a desert flower. SETH-NAKHT stands
at a distance, helmet in hand — a man who came to feed foreigners to the sea and stayed to help bury one with
honour; not redeemed, but changed. NEFER-KA, alive, watches the horizon he still has to guard.*

**MERYT:** *(beside Astrid)* What do your people call a navigator with no sea left to cross?

**ASTRID:** *(a long beat; the first true calm of her life)* I don't think my people have a word for it. *(looks
inland, at the thread of the great river)* But that river runs further than anyone here has followed it.

**MERYT:** Further than anyone's dared.

**ASTRID:** *(the ghost of Olaf's smile, and entirely her own)* Then somebody had better learn to read it. *(beat,
and she doesn't look back for anyone's nod)* Me, I think.

*She shoulders a pack, takes a last look at the cairn and the single, honest sun — and turns inland, toward the
living world she chose over the one she lost, a navigator walking toward a river instead of a sea. IPY falls in
beside her, uninvited, the way the brave do. HOLD: the two of them small against the long bright river. CUT TO
BLACK.*

**TITLE: LA PORTE DU SOLEIL.**
