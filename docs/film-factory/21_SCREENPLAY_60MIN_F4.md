# LA PORTE DU SOLEIL — full-length screenplay (Draft F4 — 60-min density)

**ODG_FILM_FACTORY_V1 · 2026-10-24 · target 60:00 · ORIGINAL fantasy-adventure.** F4 expands the gate-passed F3
(`20`, 7/10, ~20 min) to genuine ~60-min runtime by DEVELOPING scenes — crew/world/character texture, earned
antagonist turns, fuller set-pieces and aftermath — **without padding** and **without changing the approved
story, arcs, or ending**. F1/F2/F3 preserved as history. A screenplay, **NOT a film**; needs a feature critique
≥7/10 (`19`) + a human read. Original fantasy; Norse/Egyptian detail dramatized, distinct from historical fact.

**Principals:** ASTRID (19, navigator — follows, never chooses) · OLAF (60s, dying captain, her father) · HELGI
(20s, carries a wooden horse for his daughter Sǫlveig) · TOVE (20s, Astrid's closest friend; HALLA waits on a
grey beach) · MERYT (30s, river-guard captain; her forbidden dawn-rite tore the seam) · NEFER-KA (30s, reads &
can seal the seam) · IPY (10, water-carrier, mother lost to the first tide) · SETH-NAKHT (40s, garrison commander,
sustained antagonist) · the SEAM.

# ACT I — ARRIVAL (0:00–15:00)

## SEQUENCE 1 — THE CROSSING (0:00–5:30)

### 1. EXT. NORTH SEA — STORM — NIGHT
*Black water the height of hills. The longship SŌLFARI claws up a wave, sail reefed to a rag. At the steering-oar:
ASTRID, 19, reading the sea the way others read faces. Lashed to the mast, OLAF, 60s, grey as ash, too weak to
stand but still the captain every eye darts to for permission.*

**ASTRID:** She's pulling beam-on. I hold, she rolls. I fall off, I lose the next shoulder.

**OLAF:** *(thin)* And which does the water want?

**ASTRID:** *(frustrated)* I'm not asking what the water wants. I'm asking you to tell me I'm right.

**OLAF:** Ask the sea, Astrid. I've answered you for the last time tonight.

*Stung, she holds the line she doubts. The ship rolls; sea comes over the rail.*

### 2. EXT. SŌLFARI DECK — CONTINUOUS
*HELGI, 20s, ties off a snapped sheet one-handed, a child's carved wooden horse wedged in his belt. TOVE, 20s,
bails beside him, soaked and grinning at death out of habit.*

**TOVE:** When we drown, I'm telling the gods it was the navigator's nerves. Not the sea.

**HELGI:** *(not joking)* Tell them what you like. *(nods at Astrid's back)* She'll steer us true the second she
stops waiting for the old man to blink first. Trouble is she's never once done it.

**TOVE:** She will. She has to. *(beat, quieter)* I've got a beach to get back to, Helgi. I'm not dying in a story
nobody at home will even hear.

*HELGI touches the wooden horse at his belt — his own reason — and says nothing.*

### 3. EXT. NORTH SEA — THE SEAM — CONTINUOUS
*A monster wave curls to break over the ship. At its crest, where there should be foam, the WATER UNRAVELS — a
seam of hard gold light splits it, and through the gap the sea is SAND, dune, a shore under TWO SUNS. For one
impossible beat Astrid sees both worlds stacked: storm above, desert below.*

**ASTRID:** *(under her breath)* ...I can't read that.

**HELGI:** *(roaring)* THEN STEER LIKE HE'S ALREADY GONE —

*ASTRID, with no time to ask Olaf, chooses blind — throws the oar hard over, aiming the bow INTO the seam rather
than away. The ship drops through. The storm cuts out mid-roar. The keel slams into SAND. Silence, total — rain
still dripping off the rigging, hissing on hot ground.*

### 4. EXT. IMPOSSIBLE SHORE — DAWN OF TWO SUNS — CONTINUOUS
*Soaked men in a dry land under a doubled sun. The crew lift their heads, disbelieving. Behind the beached ship, a
vertical SEAM of light hangs in the air, breathing, widening a finger each minute. OLAF stirs on the litter.*

**OLAF:** *(faint)* ...You didn't ask me.

**ASTRID:** There wasn't time.

**OLAF:** *(the ghost of a smile, and something like relief)* No. There wasn't. *(his eyes close)* Remember that
it felt like that.

## SEQUENCE 2 — TWO SHORES, ONE COMMANDER (5:30–11:00)

### 5. EXT. SHORE — THE PATROL — CONTINUOUS
*Hoofbeats. River-guard crest the dune: MERYT, 30s, khopesh drawn — and behind her, where no child should be on a
patrol, IPY, 10, a clay jug on her hip. The Norse reach for weapons. ASTRID steps into the gap, hands open.*

**ASTRID:** *(slow, knowing they won't take the words, will read the hands)* We didn't cross to raid you. Your sea
reached up and took us off ours.

*MERYT doesn't lower the blade — but her eyes keep going past Astrid to the seam, and that tells Astrid everything:
the guard is more frightened of the light than of the strangers.*

**MERYT:** *(Egyptian, subtitled, to Ipy without turning)* Whatever comes out of that sky, you do not touch it.
Not this time.

**IPY:** *(Egyptian)* You said that last time. Then the sea took the market.

*MERYT flinches — a wound, quickly hidden. ASTRID clocks it: this has happened before, and the captain carries it.*

### 6. EXT. SHORE — THE FIRST TIDE — CONTINUOUS
*A low TONE rolls across the dunes. Forty paces off, flat sand SHIVERS and becomes mirror-bright SEA for three
seconds, then collapses to dune. Soldiers flinch; IPY, fearless, drifts toward it to look.*

**MERYT:** IPY — BACK —

*ASTRID is already moving — reads the water's run, cuts the angle, reaches the child as the false sea rises to
their knees, hauls her clear onto dry dune. The sea drains. ASTRID stands soaked in a desert, breathing hard.
IPY stares up at her, unafraid, assessing.*

**IPY:** *(Egyptian)* You knew where it would go.

**ASTRID:** *(gently, not understanding the words but the look)* Yeah. I did.

### 7. EXT. SHORE — SETH-NAKHT ARRIVES — CONTINUOUS
*A second, larger column — garrison soldiers, disciplined — and at their head SETH-NAKHT, 40s, commander, a man
who reads a crisis for what it can win him. He takes in the ship, the foreigners, the seam, the fear on his own
captain's face, and the drowned-child-nearly: and he sees a story he can own.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(Egyptian, subtitled, to his soldiers, for them to hear)* The sky tears, and the same morning
strangers fall out of it. The people will want to know which caused which. *(to Meryt)* Chain them.

**MERYT:** Commander — they came *through* the tear. The ghost-girl just pulled Ipy out of it.

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(mild, worse than anger)* I did not ask what is true. I asked what the town needs to see while it
is frightened. *(beat, studying Astrid)* And it needs to see that I have the thing that smells the flood before it
comes — on a leash. *(to the lieutenant)* Bring them to the garrison. The sea-ghosts are mine.

*ASTRID can't follow the words, but reads the body language exactly: the commander has made her crew his property.
She meets Meryt's eye; Meryt looks away, outranked and ashamed.*

## SEQUENCE 3 — THE GARRISON, THE RULES, THE LIE (11:00–15:00)

### 8. INT. GARRISON — COUNCIL — DUSK
*SETH-NAKHT before a nervous knot of town elders. This is new material: we see WHY he needs a scapegoat — his
authority is thin, the town is terrified, and fear is the only currency he trusts.*

**ELDER:** *(Egyptian, subtitled)* Three markets drowned this season, Commander. The people say the gods are
angry at something we have not punished.

**SETH-NAKHT:** Then we will punish something. *(smooth)* Strangers fell from the torn sky this morning. The gods
could not have sent a clearer answer. *(beat, quiet, to himself as the elders file out, almost honest)* Give them
a guilty face to hate, and they will not notice the face that cannot stop the water.

### 9. INT. GARRISON — HOLDING YARD / NEFER-KA — DAY
*The Norse held under guard. OLAF laid in shade, failing. NEFER-KA, 30s, shaved head, plain solar pectoral,
brought in — not a prisoner, but kept close; Seth-Nakht keeps his seam-guardian leashed too. He studies the
distant seam with a stillness that uncannily mirrors Astrid's way of reading water.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(to Astrid, ignoring the guards)* You read water. I have spent my life reading *that.*

**ASTRID:** Then tell me how to make it give my father's ship back.

**NEFER-KA:** It is an alignment — the two suns draw close, the wall between their ages thins, things fall through.
The debt must be repaid, or both ages bleed together until neither keeps its own rules. It closes only when
someone who can read it walks into it and shuts it from within. *(beat)* It does not return that someone.

**ASTRID:** *(too fast, filing "from within")* And if no one walks in?

**NEFER-KA:** Then the bleed grows until there is no shore left to argue it on. *(watches her)* You are already
asking the wrong question — *how do I use it* — instead of the right one: *what will it cost whoever does.*

### 10. INT. GARRISON — SETH-NAKHT, NEFER-KA, MERYT — DUSK
*The commander turns a captured Norse brooch in his fingers like a man pricing it.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** You have guarded that tear your whole life, Nefer-Ka, and it has never torn like this. Why now?

**NEFER-KA:** The alignment makes a seam possible. But this one opened wide, and early, before the suns had met.
That is not the alignment. That is a hand.

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(looking straight at Meryt)* A hand. *(lets it hang)* Whose?

**MERYT:** The priests would know better than a river-captain, Commander.

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(smiling)* Find me the hand, guardian. A guilty hand is a gift — the people forgive a ruler
anything if he gives them someone to blame. The foreigners will do until you find better.

*He has, without knowing it, named Meryt's crime as the thing that will save his standing. NEFER-KA sees her go
still. He says nothing — yet.*

### 11. INT. GARRISON — OLAF & ASTRID — NIGHT
*The crew against a wall under guard; TOVE whets a blade, HELGI turns the wooden horse. Apart, ASTRID sits with
OLAF, a torch guttering, the seam faint through a high window. This is the film's emotional spine — given room.*

**OLAF:** When you were small you'd stand at the steerboard and call the weather before I did. Every time. And
every time you'd look at me after, to see if you were allowed to be right.

**ASTRID:** You were the captain.

**OLAF:** I was your father. That's worse. *(a breath that costs him)* I heard the guardian. "From within." I saw
your face find a route and hide it from me.

**ASTRID:** *(not denying it)* If there's a way home in that light, I'll read it.

**OLAF:** Read it to get the crew home. Read it to get Helgi back to his girl and Tove to her beach. *(grips her
hand)* But if you ever walk into a thing you can't walk out of — let it be a choice you make, not a grave you dig
for me. Promise me that much.

**ASTRID:** *(a long silence — she won't lie to him)* ...Sleep, Father.

*OLAF studies her — he knows exactly what the silence means — and lets his eyes close. Through the window, the
seam widens. A soldier's shadow falls across them: Seth-Nakht's watch, always there now. END ACT I.*

# ACT II — THE BLEED (15:00–42:00)

## SEQUENCE 4 — THE LEASH (15:00–21:00)

### 12. INT. GARRISON — SETH-NAKHT'S TERMS — MORNING
*ASTRID brought before SETH-NAKHT; MERYT translating; NEFER-KA present. He offers not freedom but a leash.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(via Meryt)* My guardian says the tear widens and will drown my towns. You see where water will
go. So you will walk the shore and keep my people dry, and I will not feed your crew to the thing to calm a mob.
That is the whole of the kindness I have.

**ASTRID:** And my father? My ship?

**SETH-NAKHT:** My guests. Under my roof. *(thin smile)* A man keeps his guests close when he wants a borrowed
animal to come home at night.

*ASTRID reads every inch of it. She looks at OLAF, at her crew — all held — and nods once, hating it.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(low, only to her, after)* He keeps what you love so your gift stays his.

**ASTRID:** *(flat)* I worked that out before he finished smiling. Teach me the light. That's the one thing he
can't hold.

### 13. EXT. DESERT — THE LESSON, AND A FAILURE — DAY
*New material that EARNS her gift rather than asserting it. NEFER-KA teaches the seam's grain, a soldier
shadowing. ASTRID reads a coming tide — and gets it WRONG; she calls a flood east, it breaks west, and only
NEFER-KA's hand yanks a shepherd clear in time. She's shaken — the first time her reading has failed anyone.*

**ASTRID:** I read it east. It went west. *(rattled)* At home the sea never lied to me like that.

**NEFER-KA:** The sea at home kept one set of rules. This keeps two, badly, and changes which mid-breath. You
cannot read it the way you read a thing you trust. You have to read it the way you'd read a man who might betray
you — watching for the moment it decides to.

**ASTRID:** *(quiet)* I've never trusted anything enough to watch it that close. Not without my father's eyes
telling me I saw right.

**NEFER-KA:** Then learn. His eyes are closing, and the light does not wait for a captain's nod.

### 14. EXT. DESERT — SHE LEARNS — LATER
*ASTRID tries again — this time reading the seam as the critique asked, watching for its "betrayal." She calls a
tide, hesitates, then TRUSTS herself and re-calls it to a different line. It breaks exactly where she re-called.
Her first reading owned entirely by her. NEFER-KA notes the change.*

**NEFER-KA:** That one you didn't look at me after.

**ASTRID:** *(surprised at herself)* ...No. I didn't.

### 15. EXT. GARRISON COURTYARD — ONE BAND — NIGHT
*New material: a shared fire — Norse crew and off-duty river-guard, wary, then thawing. TOVE trades a Norse
drinking song for an Egyptian one; IPY teaches HELGI a clapping game; MERYT and ASTRID sit a little apart. This
scene PAYS OFF the ending's "one band" and the cost of Helgi's death later.*

**MERYT:** Your people sing about drowning a great deal.

**TOVE:** *(overhearing, grinning)* We sing about it so it doesn't happen. You sing about rivers so they stay in
their banks. Same prayer, different water.

**MERYT:** *(to Astrid, quieter, watching Ipy laugh with Helgi)* That child lost her mother to the first tide.
She laughs anyway. I don't know how she does it and I can't look straight at it.

**ASTRID:** She looks at things straight. My father said keep her close. *(beat)* Why can't you look at her,
Meryt?

*MERYT doesn't answer. The fire pops. The thread tightens without a word.*

## SEQUENCE 5 — THE MARKET, AND THE CREDIT (21:00–27:00)

### 16. EXT. RIVER-TOWN MARKET — DAY
*IPY shows ASTRID her world, proud. She points to a flat paving stone.*

**IPY:** *(Egyptian, subtitled)* My mother sold sweet water there. The first morning the sea walked, it walked
here. It took the corner. And her. *(sets her jug straight on the stone)* I keep her place.

**ASTRID:** *(crouching to her level)* My mother went to the sea too. Before I was old enough to carry a jug.
*(beat)* I keep her place by reading the water she's in. You keep yours by standing on it. *(IPY considers this,
and accepts her — the film's warmest beat.)*

### 17. EXT. MARKET — THE SECOND TIDE & SETH-NAKHT'S THEATRE — CONTINUOUS
*The TONE — deeper. ASTRID reads it in a heartbeat.*

**ASTRID:** *(roaring, pointing)* The east row — get them OFF it — NOW!

*MERYT bellows it in Egyptian; the crowd scatters as the east row BECOMES SEA, stalls spinning under. Where Astrid
read clear, people live. The water drains. But SETH-NAKHT is already stepping into the saved square with heralds,
arms wide, claiming it —*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(to the crowd)* Your commander's foreign hounds smell the flood before it comes! Fear nothing
while I hold their leash!

*The crowd cheers HIM. ASTRID, soaked, watches a man wear the thing she did.*

### 18. EXT. MARKET — ASTRID OUTMANEUVERS HIM — CONTINUOUS
*New material that EARNS the antagonist's later crack: Astrid doesn't rage — she learns the game Meryt named
("the one seen to save them owns them") and plays it back. As Seth-Nakht basks, she steps to the crowd, takes
IPY's hand, and speaks — letting Meryt translate loudly, truthfully —*

**ASTRID:** *(to the crowd, even)* Your commander holds no leash. The water answers the reading, and the reading
is hers — *(points to Meryt)* — and mine. He stood where it was already safe. Ask him to stand where I say the
next one breaks, and watch if he dares.

*A murmur. SETH-NAKHT's smile freezes — publicly cornered for the first time. He covers it, but he has learned
that the ghost-girl can take a crowd from him. From here he fears her, which is the seed of both his cruelty and
his eventual crack.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(quiet, to his lieutenant, watching her)* She is more useful and more dangerous than I thought.
Tighten the guard on the old man. A dangerous animal comes home faster when it fears for its master.

## SEQUENCE 6 — MIDPOINT: THE LEVERAGE (27:00–32:00)

### 19. INT. GARRISON — THE SEIZURE — NIGHT
*Nefer-Ka's finding has reached Seth-Nakht: the seam was forced by a hand; it closes with a life given. He acts,
and the floor drops out. Soldiers move OLAF's litter under heavier guard; the crew are bound.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(to Astrid, via Meryt, pleasant)* My guardian says the tear was forced open by a hand, and closes
only when a life is given to it. I have decided the life will be a *guilty* one — the hand that opened it. The
people will love the justice. *(leans in)* You will read me and my guardian to the tear's root at the convergence.
If your reading falters, or your crew grows brave, your father does not see another ugly double dawn.

*ASTRID's face. The leash just became a noose around OLAF.*

### 20. INT. GARRISON — ASTRID & MERYT — LATER
**MERYT:** He means to find the guilty hand and feed it to the seam at the convergence. He thinks a guilty life
closes it cleaner.

**ASTRID:** Does it? A guilty life?

**MERYT:** *(a beat too long)* It closes when someone who can *read* it shuts it from within. Guilt is Seth-
Nakht's idea, not the seam's. *(looks away)* But if he needs a guilty hand to throw, and mine is the guilty one —
I could spare a great many people by being the hand.

**ASTRID:** *(sharp)* That's not a plan. That's your grief finally finding a door it's allowed to walk through.

*MERYT doesn't deny it — the seed of her near-sacrifice, planted.*

### 21. INT. GARRISON — MERYT'S BROTHER — NIGHT
*New material giving Meryt's guilt real weight before the confession. ASTRID finds MERYT at a small shrine, a
single reed boat set in a bowl of water.*

**MERYT:** My brother. The river took him in flood — no body, no cairn, nothing to grieve but water. *(sets the
reed boat drifting in the bowl)* The morning of the turning I lit the dawn-rite as duty asks. And then I lit it a
second time. For him. Forbidden. To beg the river back just once. *(her hand shakes the water)* The suns touched
twice. The sky tore wide. Every corner the sea has taken since — Ipy's mother — I called it down trying to un-
drown one man.

**ASTRID:** *(no cruelty)* And now you'd let a commander throw you in to make it even.

**MERYT:** Wouldn't you? If the drowning were yours to answer for?

**ASTRID:** *(a long beat)* I don't know yet. I think I'm about to find out what I'd walk into. *(quiet)* But not
for his theatre, Meryt. If anyone walks into that light, it won't be to settle your account for a man who wants a
crowd.

## SEQUENCE 7 — THE CANYON (32:00–37:00)

### 22. EXT. DESERT CANYON — DUSK
*The forced march to the seam-root, under guard, through a canyon where the bleed is worst: waves of seawater hang
FROZEN mid-break, bronze-green, over dry rock. Norse, guard, Seth-Nakht's soldiers, OLAF's litter, IPY on TOVE's
shoulders. Beauty and dread.*

**NEFER-KA:** Walk only where I walk. Here the world has forgotten which age it stands in.

**TOVE:** *(craning up at a frozen wave the size of a hall)* If I die in this place I die owing a woman an
explanation she's waited a year for. *(to Astrid)* You're getting us to her. Say it.

**ASTRID:** *(can't lie to Tove)* I'm going to get *you* to her, Tove. Her name first, so I know who I'm aiming
you at.

**TOVE:** *(surprised into it)* ...Halla. Of Hǫrn. She mends nets and she swore she'd never love a sailor and
then she did it anyway, the fool. *(beat, hearing Astrid's pronoun)* You said "you." Not "us."

**ASTRID:** *(doesn't answer — keeps reading the path)* Mind the glass. Left of me.

### 23. EXT. CANYON — THE ECHO — CONTINUOUS
*A time-echo rolls through. For seconds, each person walks beside a translucent DOUBLE a few heartbeats behind —
their own near-future. ASTRID's double, behind her, mouths OLAF's words from scene 11: "...a choice you make, not
a grave you dig for me." She falters. And SETH-NAKHT's echo shows HIM, older, alone on a throne of sand — and the
commander flinches from it, the first human thing we've seen him do. NEFER-KA sees him see it.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(low, to Astrid)* Even he gets shown what he's closest to losing. *(beat)* Watch him. A man who
has just met the worst version of himself is a man who can still turn — or harden for good.

## SEQUENCE 8 — THE COST (37:00–39:30)

### 24. EXT. SEAM-ROOT APPROACH — THE THIRD TIDE — DAY
*The root rears ahead: a vertical wound of light. HELGI hauls an anchor line to steady the crossing. The TONE —
the biggest surge yet. The false sea rises between HELGI and dry ground, chest-high in a breath.*

**ASTRID:** HELGI — drop it — take my hand — REACH —

*A stride too far, and he knows it in his face. He looks — not at her hand — at the wooden horse in his belt. He
tears it free and THROWS it to ASTRID.*

**HELGI:** Get her home — Sǫlv—

*The sea takes him. Gone. Dry dune. The horse in Astrid's fist. TOVE screams his name into a silence that doesn't
answer. IPY buries her face in Meryt's side. SETH-NAKHT, unmoved, to a lieutenant: "The tear is hungry. Good. It
will take the guilty one all the easier."*

### 25. EXT. SEAM-ROOT APPROACH — AFTER — CONTINUOUS
*ASTRID doesn't collapse. She pockets the horse; her face sets into OLAF's tiller-focus. TOVE, wrecked, grabs her.*

**TOVE:** He threw it to you. Not his own hand. Why —

**ASTRID:** Because he'd already chosen who the horse was for. He stopped trying to save himself and saved the
thing that mattered. *(beat, barely holding)* I spent my whole life not understanding that. I understand it now.

## SEQUENCE 9 — THE TURN (39:30–42:00)

### 26. EXT. SEAM-ROOT CAMP — THE CONFESSION — NIGHT
*The suns almost one. SETH-NAKHT assembles everyone to name the guilty hand and hold it for the convergence. He
points at the Norse — the easy scapegoats. MERYT steps forward and takes it out of his mouth.*

**MERYT:** *(ringing)* The hand was mine, Commander. My brother drowned; I lit the dawn-rite a second time, for
him, forbidden. The suns touched twice. I tore the sky. Not these foreigners — me.

*ASTRID expects the confession to free her crew. Instead SETH-NAKHT SMILES — handed exactly what he wanted.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(delighted, cold)* My own captain. Even better — the people will see I spare no one, not even my
own. *(to soldiers)* Hold her for the tear. At the convergence, the guilty hand goes in. *(to Astrid)* And you
will read her safely to the heart, so my justice does not miss.

### 26A. EXT. SEAM-ROOT CAMP — IPY & MERYT — NIGHT
*MERYT bound, waiting for the death Seth-Nakht has staged. IPY slips past the guards and crouches before her — the
child whose mother drowned in the first tide Meryt's rite called down. A reckoning, not a comfort.*

**IPY:** *(Egyptian, subtitled, flat and level)* The first morning the sea walked. That was your second flame.
*(beat)* My mother.

**MERYT:** *(she does not look away; she has stopped looking away)* ...Yes. Your mother. And every corner it took
since. Me.

**IPY:** *(a long child's silence, then)* Seth-Nakht wants to throw you in. *(beat)* I don't. I want you to stand
on the shore where she stood, every day, and not look away from it. That lasts longer than drowning.

*MERYT takes it like a blow she's owed. IPY sets her mother's jug down beside the bound woman — not forgiveness, a
sentence — and goes.*

### 27. EXT. SEAM-ROOT CAMP — THE TRAP CLEAR — CONTINUOUS
*MERYT seized, OLAF held, crew held. ASTRID is to be forced to walk the one person who confessed into the seam,
for a commander's theatre — believing a guilty sacrifice is needed, which Astrid now knows is a lie.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(low, urgent)* He has it wrong. The seam does not want guilt. It wants a reader, and it keeps them.
If Meryt walks in, she dies for his story and the seam still needs a reader after. There are two people here who
can read it to the heart. Her hand is not one of them.

**ASTRID:** Then he's about to spend the wrong life — and he'll keep spending them until he spends the right one.
*(looks at the convergence)* Unless someone reads it before he can stage his justice.

*The two suns slide close enough to blur toward one coin of light. Soldiers everywhere. END ACT II.*

# ACT III — THE SEAL (42:00–60:00)

## SEQUENCE 10 — CONVERGENCE UNDER SIEGE (42:00–48:30)

### 28. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — THE SUNS CONVERGING — DAY
*The two suns are nearly one searing coin. The seam is a vertical wound taller than the mast, roaring softly. The
desert flickers constantly between sand and sea. SETH-NAKHT's soldiers ring the root; MERYT is bound; OLAF's
litter and the crew held as leverage.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(via herald, to the gathered guard)* At the joining of the suns, the guilty hand goes into the
tear, and your commander closes the wound his own captain opened! *(to Astrid)* Read her path to the heart. Now.

**ASTRID:** *(not moving)* If I walk her in, she dies and the seam stays open — she can't read it shut. You'll
have spent a life for a story and you'll still be standing here when the next dawn doubles.

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(a flicker of the doubt his echo planted; buried)* Then I will spend another. I have a garrison
of hands.

**NEFER-KA:** You have two readers. Her, and me. Spend us and you have none — and no one to shut the tear the day
it opens over your own house.

### 29. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — ASTRID TAKES THE FIELD — CONTINUOUS
*She makes her move not with a blade but with the one thing only she can do — and it's the EARNED payoff of the
market scene (18) and the lesson (13–14). She calls the next three tides, out loud, to the inch, reading the
seam's "betrayals" before they come. The seas erupt and drain around the soldiers exactly as she names them. The
guard breaks in terror — their commander's "leash" is visibly nothing; the water answers HER. In the chaos TOVE
cuts MERYT free; the crew drag OLAF's litter back.*

**ASTRID:** *(to Seth-Nakht, over the roar)* You never held the leash. I did. And I just showed your own men whose
hand the water takes orders from.

*This is the protagonist cornering him — not a vision. His authority dies in front of his soldiers because SHE
killed it.*

### 30. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — SETH-NAKHT'S CHOICE — CONTINUOUS
*His theatre collapsed, his men scattering, SETH-NAKHT draws his blade on the nearest hostage — OLAF — a last grab
at control. And then it's all in front of him at once: the canyon-echo throne of sand; the ghost-girl who just
took his garrison; a surge rising toward the crew he could still let drown. He raises the blade over the dying old
man — and a time-echo of his future self, alone on that throne, flickers between him and Olaf. His hand stops.
Then he does the first ungoverned thing in the film: he SHOVES OLAF's litter clear of the rising water with his
own body, and wheels on his panicking soldiers.*

**SETH-NAKHT:** *(hoarse, no theatre left)* HOLD THE LINE FOR THE FOREIGNERS. Hold it! *(quiet, to himself)* ...
Not a throne of sand. Not that.

*Not redeemed — he spent lives to get here, and Astrid broke him before his conscience did — but he chooses,
visibly, with his hands, to stop being the man on the throne. He throws his soldiers into holding back the tides
so the readers can work.*

## SEQUENCE 11 — THE CONTESTED SEAL (48:30–54:00)

### 31. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — TWO READERS — CONTINUOUS
*The window is open; the grain goes glass-still. NEFER-KA ties back his robe and steps toward the wound of light —
his duty, his age, his death. ASTRID steps into his path. She doesn't argue. She kneels, sets HELGI's wooden horse
upright in the sand, and lays two fingers to the stilled seam, then points to a dim fold in the glare.*

**ASTRID:** The heart's there. Not where it's brightest — where it's quiet.

*The seam pulses once, confirming her to the inch — the dim fold NEFER-KA has guarded thirty years and never once
heard another name.*

**NEFER-KA:** *(undone)* Thirty years. You found it on your first convergence.

**ASTRID:** I never once trusted the water under my own feet. I'm done not trusting it. *(a look at him, level)*
Your world needs a reader standing on this shore for the next tear — and the one after. That's you. It was always
going to have to be you who stays, so it can't be you who goes.

*Her hand rests on the wooden horse. She doesn't say the rest. NEFER-KA looks from her hand to the heart she alone
found, and steps back — the hardest obedience of his life.*

### 32. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — MERYT'S REFUSAL — CONTINUOUS
*MERYT, freed, reaches her, raw.*

**MERYT:** It should be me. I opened it. Let me be the one who —

**ASTRID:** Your grief opened it. Your life won't close it — you can't read it, and I won't spend you on a
commander's idea of justice. *(gentle, echoing Ipy's sentence, 26A)* You don't get to drown your way out of this.
You stand on the shore where it happened and you don't look away. That's longer, and it's yours. Guard it with
him.

*MERYT takes it — Ipy's sentence, now confirmed by the one person walking into the light. She stays.*

## SEQUENCE 12 — WHAT THE SEA IS OWED / SINGLE SUN (54:00–60:00)

### 33. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — OLAF — CONTINUOUS
*ASTRID crosses to OLAF, nearly gone, eyes opening clear one last time.*

**ASTRID:** I can send you all home through the seam before I shut it — the ship, Tove, the grey sea. Or I close
it. Not both. The light keeps the reader who shuts it.

**OLAF:** *(the faintest smile)* The sea takes what it's owed, girl. Owe it me — not you. *(his hand finds hers)*
I spent your whole life being the eyes behind yours. Close it. Live on the far side. And say it out loud, once,
so I hear you choose before I go.

**ASTRID:** *(steady — no tremor, the first fully unforced choice of her life)* I'm closing the seam. I'm staying.
You go home without me. I chose it. No one told me to.

*OLAF's hand tightens — pride, complete — and stills. She presses her forehead to his. She does not weep yet. She
rises, pressing Helgi's horse into TOVE's hands.*

### 34. EXT. SEAM-ROOT — TOVE'S GOODBYE — CONTINUOUS
**ASTRID:** Take him to the grey sea, Tove. Take the crew home. And this — *(the horse)* — to Sǫlveig, from your
hands, not mine. And Halla of Hǫrn: tell her you didn't drown. Tell her to her face. Don't make her read it in a
story.

**TOVE:** *(breaking, fierce)* I'll tell her you're the reason I got to. *(grips her)* You said "you," not "us,"
in that canyon. I heard it then. *(beat)* Read your way to something good over here, Astrid. Promise me a thing,
once, out loud, like you mean it.

**ASTRID:** *(the ghost of Olaf's smile, entirely her own)* I promise I'll choose it. Whatever it is. Myself.

### 35. EXT. SEAM / SHORE — THE CLOSE — CONTINUOUS
*TOVE and MERYT drive the SŌLFARI at the seam. INTERCUT: the ship with the crew and OLAF's body aboard strikes the
wound of light and is PULLED through — released backward into grey storm, cold spray, home; TOVE at the oar with
the wooden horse, screaming Astrid's name once into the crossing. ASTRID steps into the wound.*

### 36. INT. THE SEAM — THE MIDDLE — CONTINUOUS
*A corridor of stilled suns, every surface a mirror of a moment she's leaving. As she reads toward the quiet
heart, the seam shows her its lies, fuller now: OLAF young and whole at a tiller, turning to tell her she steered
true; HELGI tossing a laughing Sǫlveig skyward on a grey beach; her own mother's face, finally, reaching a hand
from the water. Each one says: turn, run, come home, you've earned it. She reads THROUGH them — not flinching,
naming each as the light's lie — to the dim heart.*

**ASTRID:** *(to the vision of her mother, quiet, not stopping)* I know. I know. But I didn't choose you. I'm
choosing this.

*She closes her hand. The seam seals shut behind the vanished ship.*

### 37. EXT. SHORE — THE SUNS PART — DAWN
*The single coin splits. One sun sinks for good. The false seas drain and do not return. An ordinary dawn — one
sun — on an ordinary shore. The seam is gone. ASTRID stands alone, alive, exiled from her own age forever. One
choice, and it was the whole of her. NOW, at last, she lets herself weep — once, briefly, for all of it — then
wipes her face.*

### 38. EXT. THE CAIRN — SINGLE-SUN MORNING
*A grey-stone cairn on gold sand — built by Egyptian hands for a Norse captain whose body went home without it.
MERYT sets the last stone. IPY lays a desert flower. SETH-NAKHT stands apart, helmet in hand — a commander who
came to feed strangers to the sea and ended holding the line for them; not forgiven, but no longer the man on the
throne of sand; he gives the cairn a soldier's nod and no speech. NEFER-KA watches the horizon he still guards.*

**MERYT:** *(beside Astrid)* What do your people call a navigator with no sea left to cross?

**ASTRID:** *(a long beat; the first true calm of her life, looking at the thread of the great river)* We don't
have a word for it. *(beat)* But that river runs further than anyone here has followed it. Someone should learn to
read it.

**MERYT:** Further than anyone's dared.

**ASTRID:** *(turning inland, not looking back for anyone's nod)* Then it had better be me.

*IPY falls in beside her, uninvited, the way the brave do, her mother's jug on her hip. HOLD: the two of them
small against the long bright river, one honest sun above. CUT TO BLACK.*
